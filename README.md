# imok 1v1

A blocky three.js shooter you play in the browser against bots: 1v1, 2v2 or free for all.

## Publishing rules (read before changing anything)

The site is published by the **Check and publish** GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`). Every push to `main` is checked first; if the check fails nothing is published and the last good version stays live.

- **Run the check before every commit:** `node tools/check.js` (syntax of every script in `index.html` and `sw.js`). `node tools/check.js --smoke` also loads the page in headless Chromium and starts a Duels match (needs Playwright). The pre-commit hook runs the syntax check for you once it's switched on for the clone: `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`.
- **Never use `//` comments in `index.html`.** The code is written in long single lines, so a `//` silently turns everything after it on that line into a comment (this took the site down on 30 Oct 2026). Use `/* ... */`. The check flags a `//` with code after it.
- **Work in one place, on `main`.** Make changes in the linked local 1v1 folder (or a clone of this repo), commit to `main`, and push before finishing. Don't leave work on side branches or only as patch files. If a session can't push, it must say so plainly.
- **Pull before you start** (`git pull`), since several sessions work on this repo.

**Bot play styles** – every round each bot secretly picks one of ten styles (and weapons to match), so you have to work out what you're up against:
- **Rusher** – charges straight at you, jukes and jumps, never retreats.
- **Sniper** – takes high ground far away, scopes in, relocates after every couple of shots, switches to its sidearm if you get close.
- **Lurker** – hides out of sight near you and springs when you come close or turn your back.
- **Flanker** – circles round out of view and hits you from the side.
- **Turtle** – walls up when it sees you, peeks from cover, backs off when hurt.
- **Duelist** – strafes and trades shots at mid range.
- **Hunter** – always has a rough fix on where you are and keeps tracking you down.
- **Breacher** – shotgun; hops between hiding spots, each one closer, then bursts in.
- **Kiter** – holds its favourite range: backs off as you push, follows as you run.
- **Camper** – picks one spot with a view, walls up and holds the angle all round.

All styles push when you're low, play safer when hurt, and chase the clock late in a round when behind on health. Sniper, Turtle, Kiter and Camper aren't used in Rumble. A bot you kill comes back hunting you until it next gets a kill, but the hunt doesn't blind it: if another player shoots it, or is in plain sight within about 20 m, it deals with them first and then carries on after you (and any bot turns on whoever has hit it in the last few seconds).

**Rumble** – Duels with no guns (also 1v1, 2v2 or free for all): fists only (18 per punch), on any map (every map is available in every mode); Rumble games get no shop, armour plates or barrels. Punch someone while you're falling for 20 damage: you both get knocked back and they're stunned for a second. Abilities, walls, pick-ups, slams and finishing moves all still work.

**Simple and Normal in 2v2** – picking **2v2** under Players also shows the rules row (labelled 2v2 rules): **Normal** plays as before, **Simple** gives everyone (you, your teammate and both bots) the M4, the sword and grenades, with no loadout screen and no character abilities, exactly like Simple 1v1. The choice is shared with 1v1's, so it stays as you left it when you switch between them.

**Map and character pickers** – no list under the picture: the map box itself is the picker, every map as a small picture plus a **?** Random tile. **Street Life** is picked when the game opens. Whatever is picked has a red edge, and only that one (nothing moves on its own). Click a picture to pick it, or click **Random** so that only the Random tile is red. With Random picked, pressing **Character** sets a white glow hopping round the pictures; it slows and stops on the map it chose, which lights up in white for a moment, then you move on to the Character screen (that's the map you play; rematches roll fresh). The Character screen works the same way: one box with every fighter's portrait plus Random, the picked one edged in red with its name and ability shown above, and with Random picked, **Loadout** (or **Start** in Rumble and Simple) spins and lands on your fighter in white before moving on.

**Stamina and regen** – there's no sprinting: everyone walks 25% faster than the original walk speed instead (bots too). Walking drains stamina slowly and aiming down sights faster; stand still and it starts refilling after half a second, full again in about 3 seconds (twice as fast with the shop's Stamina). At 0 you move 25% slower until it's back to a quarter. Getting shot drains stamina too, more for harder hits (an HDR body shot takes most of it, an SMG bullet a sliver). A double jump costs 15 stamina and works whenever you have that much. Health regenerates once you've gone 3 seconds without being hit, at 10 HP a second; bots regenerate too.

**HUD** – there are no health bars over players' heads or over sentries, just their name, in their team colour (in free for all, the colour of their character); bottom left is a vitals panel with health (green), armour (blue) and stamina (purple) bars in that order, and above it a compact items row: an icon, its key and how many you have (ability, consumable, walls, plus shop grenades and speed boots when you have them); on phones this row is hidden because the buttons show the same counts. Top left is a small leaderboard of everyone in the match with kills, assists (damaged someone a teammate or rival then eliminated within 15 s; the kill always goes to whoever downed the player, and anyone else who finishes them off gets an assist) and deaths; anyone out for the current round is greyed. Top right is a round minimap that turns with you and shows the map layout, the shop (gold square) and teammates (blue); the ground fills the whole circle, with no map-edge rectangle. Enemies never show unless they fire: each shot leaves a tiny red dot where they were, fading over a couple of seconds. Whenever anyone is eliminated a small red X marks the spot for 3 seconds. While you're out, the vitals and weapon panels switch to the player you're watching (name, kills/assists/deaths, live health, armour, stamina, gun and ammo); in deathmatch you watch whoever got you until you respawn. Status effects fill the screen (no text): a grenade or rocket blast turns the edges red, splits the colours and leaves soot spots on the lens; being stunned gives wobbling double vision, a yellow haze and three cartoon stars at the top; being frozen by Frost lets frost creep in from the edges with a blue tint; a flashbang whites the screen out and leaves a fading burnt-in afterimage of what you were looking at. Anyone stunned or dazed has four inked cartoon stars spinning round above their head. Explosions (rockets, grenades, fuel drums, cars) have a white flash, a shockwave ring and dust skirt along the ground, a billowing fireball that lights up its surroundings, sparks and debris thrown out, then a dark smoke column. The screen only shakes during a finisher if you're the one being finished. When you're hit, a short thin red arc round the crosshair points to where the shot came from and fades in under a second, turning as you turn.

**Volume** – Sound, Weapons and Music sliders are in the Options panel (the gear icon, top right of the front menu); they're the same settings as the ones on the pause menu. All three follow a curve, so the low end is very quiet (20% on the slider is about 2% volume). Weapons sets gunfire, reloads, weapon swaps, launchers, the flamethrower, sword swings, near misses and explosions on top of the Sound level (so Sound at 50% and Weapons at 50% makes guns quieter than everything else); moving it fires a test M4 shot so you can hear the level. It is saved in the browser like the others.

**Pause menu** – Resume, Controls (every key, grouped, with **key rebinding** at the top on computers: click an action, press the new key; picking a key that is already used swaps the two, Esc cancels, P stays as pause, Reset keys puts them back; the written key list, the HUD items row and the menu hints follow your keys; touch buttons are unaffected), HUD layout and Home, plus settings: **Graphics** (Auto, Low, Med, High), **Frame rate** (Auto, 30, 60, Max; Auto is the default for anyone who hasn't picked one: uncapped on computers and 60 on phones, and if the game still can't hold about 45 fps once Auto graphics is as low as it goes (or graphics is set by hand) it settles on a steady 30, then about every 45 s tries the higher rate again for a few seconds and keeps it if it holds; the note under the settings and the FPS readout show what Auto is doing), **Auto weapon switch** (swap to your other gun when the one in hand runs dry; if the other gun is empty too, or you swap back to an empty gun, it reloads automatically; off by default), touch controls on/off, and on phones **Aim assist** (Off, On, Strong), **Aim when firing** (holding Fire also aims down sights) and **Gyro aim**. Sliders: camera sensitivity (up to 6×), **Aim sensitivity (other guns)** (0.1–1), **Sniper scope sensitivity** (0.1–2, HDR and Kar98), sound and music, plus **Battery saver** (30 fps and Low graphics in one tap) and **Show FPS** (a small frame-rate readout). HUD layout switches each panel on or off; **Move & resize** is where everything is placed and sized: drag any panel or control, tap it to make it bigger or smaller (60–160%) and more or less see-through (Opacity 10–100%, kill feed starts at 65%), and on phones an **All buttons** size; the edit bar can be minimised to a strip at the top. Your layout is remembered (phones keep their own layout, separate from desktop) and Reset layout puts it back. Below the volume sliders: **Field of view** (60–100°, 70 by default; aiming down sights still uses each gun's own zoom), **Brightness** (50–150%), **Crosshair** style (Cross + dot, Cross, T, Dot or Circle, with a live preview; guns with their own sight, such as the snipers' red dot and the rocket ring, keep it), **Crosshair colour** (white, green, yellow, cyan or pink; it still turns red over an enemy), **Crosshair size** (Small, Medium, Large), **Crosshair outline** (on/off), **Hit sound** and **Hitmarker** (each on/off), **Kill commentary** (the Double kill / Payback callouts, on/off) and **Reset all settings** (tap twice; puts sensitivity, volumes, graphics, touch and aim settings, these options, keys and the HUD layout back to the defaults and restarts the game; your level, records and menu picks stay). Slow motion only kicks in for moments you're part of (your own down, kill or finishing move), never for other players' finishers. XP is only shown on the menu and the end-of-match screen.

**Replays and Home** – clips are recorded from exactly what you see, scope included, at up to 1280×720 and 30 fps (cropped to 16:9, compressed in the background). Lower graphics settings record smaller, slower clips (Medium: 640 wide at 20 fps, 480 at 12 fps on phones; Low: no replays). Only the 4 best of the match are kept: the winning shot first, then finishing moves, headshots and quick double kills. Each clip keeps the moment that decided the fight: the shot that downed someone (from about 3 seconds before it to 1 second after) joined to the last 4.5 seconds before they're finally out (the finish or finishing move) and a moment after, so a long finisher or a downed opponent can't push the actual shot out of the clip. Clips are only shown at the end of the game, behind the video icon on the results bar. Watching a replay pauses the game; Save video downloads it as a video file. There's no floating Home icon any more: Home is on the pause menu and is a button beside Rematch on the end-of-match bar.

**Fists, Sword and Ninja Stars** – with fists out every character holds a boxing guard (elbows bent, fists up by the chin) and each punch snaps one arm straight out along the aim, alternating left and right with the shoulders turning into it, so punches read clearly on every character. Melee has its own sounds: a quick air whoosh and a meaty body thud for fists, a steel “shwing” and slice for the Sword, and a whirring throw, a stab on a hit and a thunk into walls for Ninja Stars.

**Damage** – every weapon and ability hits 20% harder than it used to (Gorillaz's smash up to 48; Murda Medic heals 48), so fights end a little quicker. On top of that, every primary and secondary gun (not fists, the sword, abilities or lethals) does 25% more damage per hit, rounded to whole numbers; the loadout cards show the new figures. Both boosts are now baked into the `GUNS` table in `index.html`, so the `dmg` values there are the real in-game numbers. **Balance pass (Nov 2026):** the AKM is the long-range AR (full damage out to 55 m, dropping to 45% by 130 m, 0.14 s between shots; 4 body shots in 0.42 s); the Shotty Horror's second barrel waits 0.45 s; the UMP hits for 18 (6 body shots); the Dual Pistols fire every 0.16 s and the Vector every 0.045 s, so secondaries no longer out-kill the primaries up close.

**Crossbow and breakables** – the Crossbow's bolt flies at 200 m/s with only a slight drop (it used to be 75 m/s, slow enough that a moving target had stepped out of the way before it arrived), leaving a faint green streak. Every weapon can break things: bolts and Ninja Stars smash glass, lamps and barrels, set off mines and knock loose items along their path; the Sword and Fists smash glass, barrels and other breakables at head or waist height when a swing meets no one; and the Flamethrower burns through glass and props and sets off barrels where its jet lands (guns and explosives already did).

**Reviving** – stand over a downed teammate and they glow green and revive twice as fast (2.5 seconds instead of 5).

**End of match** – "You caught the W!" or "Took the L this time :(" in big letters, the score and four stats: Kills, Damage, Accuracy, Headshots. Rematch and, if any clips were saved, a video icon (with the number of clips on it) that pops up beside it; tap it for the list of clips. Clips are only offered at the end of the game, never between rounds. If you pick nothing within 10 seconds (a small "Home in Ns" countdown shows on the right) it takes you back to the home screen; clicking anywhere on the bar stops the countdown. Home is the button beside Rematch.

**Lethals** (what used to be called consumables; press G): Stun Grenade, plus the ones below. Grenade and HeartBeat have been taken out of the loadout, and Stim has been removed from the game completely (Simple 1v1s still give everyone grenades).
- **Flash Bang** (2 per round) – thrown like a grenade; anyone who can see the blast gets a white screen for exactly 1 second including the fade-out (0.6 if facing away). Bots lose track of you for as long.
- **Proximity Mine** (2 per round) – laid on the floor in front of you, armed after a second (fast-blinking red light while arming), goes off when an enemy comes within about 1.7 m (60 damage); anyone can shoot it to set it off: a full mine blast with the same damage and radius, counted as the shooter's, so shooting an enemy's mine next to them hurts them, and you take half if you're inside the blast; it's dark grey with a dim red blink, so it's easy to miss. Other blasts set it off too.
- **Cluster Grenade** (2 per round) – a small burst that throws out five bomblets, each a smaller, weaker blast (24 damage) a moment later.
- **Gold Armour** (always on) – you start every round or respawn with 30 armour, which rebuilds back to 30 slowly once you've gone 3 seconds without being hit. Your armour bar (and the sparks when it soaks a hit) is gold instead of blue.

Menu descriptions are short: Stun "Stunned 1s", Flash "Blinded 1s", Mine "Proximity", Cluster "Multi BOOM!!!", Gold Armour "Armour + Rebuilds".

**Character motion** (looks only: aim, speed and hitboxes are unchanged) – legs are driven by where the feet should be: each foot plants and travels back at ground speed, then lifts and swings forward, in whichever direction you're moving (forwards, backwards or sideways), with a pelvis bob, a lean into the run and into starts and stops, a tuck in the air and a knee-bend on landing that grows with the fall. The chest shares the aim pitch through a waist joint (hips go the other way so the head stays over its hitbox), breathes at rest and flinches away from each hit, with the head snapping on headshots. Feet are planted at a point in the world while they are down, so they don't slide through turns, stops or changes of speed; each swing leaves and lands near ground speed; after a stop or a turn on the spot a foot that is out of place takes a small step back under the hip; knees ease into full stretch; the stride's shape follows a smoothed speed so running into a wall doesn't snap the pose; other fighters' bodies turn towards their aim over a few frames. The game simulates 60 times a second, and drawn positions (every body, and your camera with yours) are blended between steps so movement is even on 90, 120 and 144 Hz screens. Going down is a short collapse that settles into a prone crawl (arms pulling in turn, legs trailing). Shots at a downed player count if they hit the fixed downed box or the body you can actually see (head, chest, hips, knees and feet), so the collapse, a raised head or a body turned away from a wall can't make hits miss. Models get a rim light, a contact shadow under the feet, surface relief worked out from their own texture and a small brightness lift for very dark outfits. Per-character extras live in `CHAR_FX` in `index.html` (Wednesday has springy hair, a swinging trouser chain, loose hems and a blink). `CFX` switches each part off for testing.

**Deaths and celebrations** – an ordinary kill drops the body as a ragdoll (it crumples under its own weight, is shoved the way the killing shot or blast was travelling, and folds over or against whatever it lands on) with a few small red blocks round it (no explosion, no slow motion); only finishing moves blow people apart. Downing someone no longer slows the game either. If you finish a downed enemy while every one of their teammates is also down, the whole team is out. Winning a round gets a quick fist pump, a high five between teammates or a little jig, except when a finishing move ends the round: then there's no celebration and the next round starts sooner. Winning the match cuts away from the map to a black throne room: the winners sit down on gold-and-red-velvet thrones under warm spotlights, and each one's kills and damage for the match fade in on the throne back above their head, two blonde ring girls (black one-pieces, red belts, red lips, sparkly silver heels) walk in carrying jewelled crowns and place them on their heads (the crown sits on the top of the head or hair, flames and spikes poke through it), then the camera cuts to a close-up of each winner turning to the lens with a raised eyebrow and a smirk, before going back to the wide shot, which stays behind the results screen. No coloured lights or rings. Space or Enter skips it. Before any celebration or finishing move starts, anyone standing close to a wall, car, prop or other solid object first steps out into clear space (finishers slide both players into open room in under half a second), so nobody clips into or through anything. Round and match results sit in a slim bar across the top so the celebration stays in view. The kill feed sits under the score at 65% opacity.

**Explosive drums** – every Duels map (and any new one) gets a few small clusters of red fuel drums (one to three drums each, spread well apart), tucked beside walls, crates and cars on open ground, clear of spawns, shops, ladders, stairs, water and the train lines. Shoot one and it blows (and sets off its neighbours). They sit in the same spots each time you play a map.

**Bots get everywhere you can on foot** – bots climb ladders, jump and double-jump up onto crates, containers, ledges and roofs (onto a canopy roof like the shop they jump straight up beside the edge and step on once clear), and squeeze through tight gaps beside railings. A jump a bot keeps missing gets dropped from its routes for the rest of the match. Rooftop spawns are only placed where bots can climb to. The only spots they can't reach are ones you need Jet's rocket boost for (cliff-top rocks, the pavilion and signal-box roofs, the crane cab top, the platform shelter roofs).

**Bots pick the right gun for the range** – bots swap to whichever of their two guns suits the distance: shotguns, SMGs and other close-range guns when you're up close, rifles, snipers and LMGs further out (never a rocket at point blank, and they think twice before swapping to an empty gun). How quickly and how reliably they do it depends on difficulty: Easy bots rethink every few seconds and often stay on the wrong gun, Normal ones every second or two, and Hard and Extreme ones swap almost as soon as the range changes.

**Bots under fire** – when a bot gets shot it picks a reaction instead of always walling up: dodging side to side with hops, ducking into cover, backing off, relocating round your side, walling up (less often) or just fighting on. Rushers and breachers mostly fight on.

**Armour plates** – a few glowing blue armour plates sit on the hardest places to get to on each map (high roofs on Street Life, the top of the book tower on Toy Town); Rumble games have none, on any map, since Rumble has no armour. Plates always sit in different areas of the map from each other. Collect one for +50 armour (up to 100); it comes back 60 seconds later somewhere else (away from where it was and from the other plates), and every round start shuffles them to fresh hard-to-reach spots. A plate on a building that gets brought down moves elsewhere after the same 60 seconds. Armour doesn't carry over: everyone starts each round with none.

**Cover** – bullets go through wood (fences, sheds, furniture, wooden floors and crates; each layer takes off 30% of the damage, up to three layers) but not through concrete, brick, walls, cars or built walls. Bodies can't pass through solid structures: anyone pushed into a wall is moved back out.

At 0 HP you go down instead of dying, with 30 downed health that takes full damage, so a few shots finish you: you crawl slowly, and if nobody finishes you, you revive automatically after 5 seconds with whatever downed health you have left, and regen waits 3 seconds before it starts. Downed players can't punch (bots included). While down, clicking fire tags the enemy you're aiming at so they're outlined in red for 3 seconds. In 2v2, if you're down and your teammate is eliminated, you're out too. The opponent can walk up and press F for a finishing move picked at random each time from every finishing move in the game (Pancake comes up twice as often as the others), never the same one twice in a row. Every finishing move has its own sound effects. Knockout, Sky drop and Combat (harpoon them in close, then uppercut) end by throwing the body right out of the arena: off the roof on Sky High, into the sea on Lagoon, or out over the fences, picking a direction and enough height to clear whatever is in the way; it smashes or splashes where it lands. Finish someone close to the arena's fence, railing or rope and the move is always Pancake, Knockout or Sky drop, and they go out over it (Pancake's last swing hurls them out instead of flattening them). Every elimination plays the winner's kill effect: each character has two kill effects of their own. XP: +10 per round won, +5 per round lost, 100 XP per level. Built walls sink away after 6 seconds. Walls are easy to shoot down: a hit nudges a brick out along the shot, a broken brick is knocked out, drops and bursts when it hits the ground, any brick left with nothing under it falls too, and knocking out two bricks from the bottom row brings the whole wall down, toppling away from the shots.

The hip-fire crosshair is white and turns red when an enemy is under it, in clear view and within the weapon's range. Aiming down sights with any non-sniper gun switches to first person with see-through ring sights (bigger rings on ARs and LMGs, small on SMGs and pistols, huge on the rocket launcher). Most secondaries have their own sight and zoom: the Uzi an open U-notch with gold dots and little zoom, the Vector an EOTech-style holo box with a cyan ring reticle, the P90 a round smoked-green ring sight with a triangle-in-ring reticle, the UMP a T2-style red-dot tube with the tightest zoom, the Sting no sights at all (the gun cants low and right and a teal chevron shows where you'll hit), the Rocket Launcher a full-screen amber RPG optic with range chevrons that turns red, with a pulsing ring and a TARGET range tag, when an enemy within 80 m is under the crosshair, and the Swarm a lock-on display whose brackets snap onto the enemy under your crosshair with pips for the missiles left in the salvo (on a lock the brackets, frame and LOCK tag turn red and pulse) (the MP5 and Dual Pistols keep their rings); bigger guns take longer to raise (0.12s Dual Pistols up to 0.42s Rocket Launcher) and only reach full accuracy once the sights are up. SMGs and pistols hit hardest up close but fall off fast; ARs hit harder per shot but fire slower. ARs and LMGs zoom in further when aiming down sights (about 2x) for longer-range shots. The HDR and Kar98 aim through a scope (the HDR's view is a little tighter) and fire on release: hold fire to scope in, let go to shoot (if you let go before the bolt has cycled it fires the moment it's ready); a small red ✕ under the crosshair (or right-click on desktop) cancels without firing, each with a red centre dot and a thin outline in the gun's colour. The shop's Scope adds a see-through red-dot ring sight to the AKM, M4, Fireblade, MP5 and Uzi. Every gun card in the loadout shows a small grey class tag in the top-right corner of its picture (AR, LMG, Sniper, Shotgun, SMG, Pistols, Launcher, Crossbow, Melee). Weapons: pick one primary – HDR sniper (long range, a headshot downs in one, 2 body shots), Kar98 (short-range sniper; white with a black scope so it stands apart from the black HDR), AKM, M4 (gold; harder hits, slower fire) or Fireblade (LMG) – and one secondary – MP5, Uzi (heavier, steadier SMG), Vector, P90, **UMP** (blue long-range SMG with a built-in red dot and see-through mag; 15 damage, about 10 shots a second, 25 rounds, damage holds to about 20 m), **Sting** (teal Skorpion-style machine pistol; the fastest-firing gun at about 28 shots a second, 8 damage, 20 rounds, near-perfect hip fire and the quickest swap), Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher (slow-firing splash), **Swarm** (orange micro-missile launcher: each pull ripples out 4 mini rockets that curve onto whatever you're aiming at, or follow the enemy under your crosshair; 15 splash damage each, 2 salvos then a 3 s reload) or Sword.

**Gun models** – every gun is drawn in a realistic style (`realGun` in `index.html`): real proportions and parts, the gun's own colour as an anodised or cerakote finish on the metal, black polymer or grained walnut furniture, parkerised steel small parts, engraved markings, and no glow or cartoon outline (only sight dots, lenses and LEDs light up). Fourteen are full rebuilds (M4, AKM, FAMAS, Fireblade, M60, HDR, Kar98, SPAS-12, Shotty Horror, MP5, UMP, Sting, Vector, Uzi); the Crossbow, P90, Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher, Swarm and Flamethrower keep their shape with the realistic finish. The M4 set the extra finish: polished gold small parts and bolt, a black-chrome barrel, scroll engraving and a gold mag plate; a round counter on the receiver that shows your live ammo in first person (red on the last 5 rounds); and a muzzle that glows hot the more you fire and cools again (first person only; always warm in the loadout picture). Colours: the FAMAS and Shotty Horror are polished silver, the Vector and P90 are black (the P90 with a silver barrel), the HDR has a polished silver barrel, and the Fireblade has swept flame paint over its red receiver instead of scrollwork. The Dual Pistols have engraved slides and hot muzzles. The UMP's weapon light is gone. The Kar98 keeps its white stock but is framed in black (recoil pad, cheek riser, grip and forend panels, a framed butt panel, vented handguard and forend cap), with a black-chrome barrel and ported muzzle brake, a box magazine with a gold base, a big black bolt knob on a gold stem, gold scope rings, an open trigger guard with a gold trigger, and the hot muzzle; its iron sights and cleaning rod are gone. Lethals (press G; for grenades, hold G to see a clean dotted throw arc that stops at the first thing it would hit, with a circle there (the grenade itself still bounces and rolls as before), release to throw toward your crosshair; aiming with Ninja Stars shows their flight path too): Grenade (2 per round, 60 splash damage; thrown a long way, it explodes on anyone it hits, otherwise 2 seconds after it first lands, in a fireball with smoke and a scorch mark), Stun Grenade (2 per round; anyone caught in it moves at 25% speed for 3 seconds, 1.5 if you catch yourself, but can still shoot) or HeartBeat (see the enemy through walls for 6s, 25s cooldown). The bot gets a random one and uses it too. You also get four buildable walls per round. Magazines are small; reloads are quick, an empty gun in your hands starts reloading by itself after a short beat (even if you switched weapons mid-reload), and R reloads early.

Controls: WASD move · Space jump (press again in mid-air to double jump; it costs 15 stamina) · mouse aim (click to lock) · left click fire (snipers: hold to scope, release to fire) · right click aim down sights / scope · C crouch (toggle; moving forward or jumping stands you up) · E ability · G consumable · T shop grenades (when your consumable isn't the grenade) · V put down a shop Sentry · F finish · R reload · 1/2/3 or wheel switch (primary, secondary, fists; fists are always there as a 3rd option and you move 20% faster with them out, except in Rumble where they are all you have; the touch Swap button cycles all three) · Q build wall · P or Esc pause (controls and sensitivity sliders are on the pause menu). Touch controls switch on automatically on phones and tablets and can be toggled on the pause menu (see **Phones** below).

**Play a friend (online)** – on the front menu, open the **Play a friend** icon (top right); **Host game** gives you a 4-letter room code; your friend opens the game, types the code and presses **Join**. The friend's character and guns are whatever they pick on their menu; the host chooses the mode and map and presses Start, and the friend takes the place of the first enemy bot (in 1v1 you fight each other; in 2v2 and Free for all the other slots stay bots). The host's game runs the match; the friend's movement and shots show instantly on their screen and the host decides the hits. If the friend leaves or drops out, a bot takes over their fighter and they can rejoin with the same code. Connections go device to device (WebRTC via the free PeerJS matchmaking server); a few strict mobile or work networks may block the direct link.

**Secondaries** are the MP5, Uzi, Vector, P90, Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher and Sword (the Ninja Stars are out of the loadout and the bots' kits).

**Vector** – one of the fastest-firing guns (about 22 rounds a second, 14 damage a bullet, 25-round mag, so it empties in about a second); almost no kick but the spread opens up fast if you hold the trigger. Hot-pink "Super V" lower under a flat-top gunmetal upper with a full rail, flip sights, weapon light, slotted shroud and brake, and a folding skeleton stock.

**P90** – 50-round bullpup (15 damage a bullet) with hip-fire almost as tight as aiming; slower to aim and a 1.3 s reload. Sculpted charcoal body with neon green trim, trigger and thumbhole cut-outs, a smoked see-through top mag showing the rounds lying crossways and an orange follower, a rounded ring sight and a flash hider.

**Shotty Horror** (replaces the AA-12) – a side-by-side double-barrelled shotgun (break action, top lever, twin gold triggers, cyan stock with a pink glow strip, gold bead sight): two shells, fired one after the other (0.45 s apart), then a 1.2 s reload. Each shell is 10 pellets of 13 damage (130 if they all land within 7 m; damage drops off to 45% by 20 m). It's a two-tap gun: a single shell tops out at 75% of their health, so it takes both barrels to down someone; only an absolutely point-blank shell (within 1.5 m, at least half the pellets hitting) downs in one.

**Phones** – landscape only (a "Turn your phone sideways" screen shows in portrait and pauses the match). Move stick on the left, drag anywhere to look (fast swipes turn further), and a thumb-reach arc of buttons round Fire: Aim, Jump, Crouch, Reload, Swap, Ability, Wall and Item, with Finish and Nade appearing only when you can use them; counts and cooldowns show on the buttons. Add the game to your home screen for full screen (Android also goes full screen and locks to landscape when a game starts; iPhone shows a one-time Add to Home Screen tip). Sounds, icons and three.js are cached for quick loading. Phones start on Medium graphics with a 60 fps cap; menus, pause and results screens drop to 20 fps to keep the phone cool. Small props aren't drawn far away on Medium and Low, and characters are built from about half as many pieces as before. Auto starts recent high-end phone GPUs on High, and after it has had to drop a level it tries one level higher again next time. Switching away from the app pauses the match. On phone menus the Start button floats in the corner.

**Parkour** – 12 obstacle courses with double jump, swings and moving platforms (switched off in the menu for now; the code is still there).

Static scenery is merged into a handful of meshes at load (instead of ~1,000 separate boxes, each with its own outline), which keeps the frame rate up on Maple Street.

Under the hood the match runs on a fixed 60-ticks-a-second clock on every device, player input reaches the game as one command per tick, shot spread uses a per-match seed, and the match state can be saved as a small snapshot: groundwork for online multiplayer.

It's a single static `index.html` (three.js and fonts load from CDNs), served by GitHub Pages. To play locally, open `index.html` in a browser.
