# imok 1v1

A blocky three.js shooter you play in the browser against bots: 1v1, 2v2 or free for all.

## Publishing rules (read before changing anything)

The site is published by the **Check and publish** GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`). Every push to `main` is checked first; if the check fails nothing is published and the last good version stays live.

- **Run the check before every commit:** `node tools/check.js` (syntax of every script in `index.html` and `sw.js`). `node tools/check.js --smoke` also loads the page in headless Chromium and starts a Duels match (needs Playwright). The pre-commit hook runs the syntax check for you once it's switched on for the clone: `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`.
- **Never use `//` comments in `index.html`.** The code is written in long single lines, so a `//` silently turns everything after it on that line into a comment (this took the site down on 30 Oct 2026). Use `/* ... */`. The check flags a `//` with code after it.
- **Work in one place, on `main`.** Make changes in the linked local 1v1 folder (or a clone of this repo), commit to `main`, and push before finishing. Don't leave work on side branches or only as patch files. If a session can't push, it must say so plainly.
- **Pull before you start** (`git pull`), since several sessions work on this repo.

**Bot play styles** – every round each bot secretly picks one of ten styles (and weapons to match), so you have to work out what you're up against:
- **Rusher** – charges straight at you, jukes and jumps, never retreats.
- **Sniper** – takes high ground far away, scopes in, relocates after every couple of shots, switches to its sidearm if you get close.
- **Lurker** – hides out of sight near you and springs when you come close or turn your back.
- **Flanker** – circles round out of view and hits you from the side.
- **Turtle** – walls up when it sees you, peeks from cover, backs off when hurt.
- **Duelist** – strafes and trades shots at mid range.
- **Hunter** – always has a rough fix on where you are and keeps tracking you down.
- **Breacher** – shotgun; hops between hiding spots, each one closer, then bursts in.
- **Kiter** – holds its favourite range: backs off as you push, follows as you run.
- **Camper** – picks one spot with a view, walls up and holds the angle all round.

All styles push when you're low, play safer when hurt, and chase the clock late in a round when behind on health. Sniper, Turtle, Kiter and Camper aren't used in Rumble.

**Rumble** – Duels with no guns (also 1v1, 2v2 or free for all): fists only (18 per punch), on the small Backyard map or The House. Punch someone while you're falling for 20 damage: you both get knocked back and they're stunned for a second. Abilities, walls, pick-ups, slams and finishing moves all still work.

Online, only the host sees sentries for now. Unused grenades, gold included, are lost if your side loses the round. After a pick the shop restocks for you in 30 seconds, and it is always restocked at the start of a round. If the round timer runs out, the side with the most health plus armour left wins the round. Bots shop too, but rarely: their first chance comes 40–70 seconds in, then every 60–100 seconds, and only half the time, when you're not close and in view. **Stamina and regen** – there's no sprinting: everyone walks 25% faster than the original walk speed instead (bots too). Walking drains stamina slowly and aiming down sights faster; stand still and it starts refilling after half a second, full again in about 3 seconds (twice as fast with the shop's Stamina). At 0 you move 25% slower until it's back to a quarter. Getting shot drains stamina too, more for harder hits (an HDR body shot takes most of it, an SMG bullet a sliver). A double jump costs 15 stamina and works whenever you have that much. Health regenerates once you've gone 3 seconds without being hit, at 10 HP a second; bots regenerate too.

**HUD** – there are no health bars over players' heads or over sentries, just their name, in their team colour (in free for all, the colour of their character); bottom left is a vitals panel with health (green), armour (blue) and stamina (purple) bars in that order, and above it a compact items row: an icon, its key and how many you have (ability, consumable, walls, plus shop grenades and speed boots when you have them); on phones this row is hidden because the buttons show the same counts. Top left is a small leaderboard of everyone in the match with kills, assists (damaged someone a teammate or rival then eliminated within 15 s; the kill always goes to whoever downed the player, and anyone else who finishes them off gets an assist) and deaths; anyone out for the current round is greyed. Top right is a minimap that turns with you and shows the map layout, the shop (gold square) and teammates (blue). Enemies never show unless they fire: each shot leaves a tiny red dot where they were, fading over a couple of seconds. Whenever anyone is eliminated a small red X marks the spot for 3 seconds. While you're out, the vitals and weapon panels switch to the player you're watching (name, kills/assists/deaths, live health, armour, stamina, gun and ammo); in deathmatch you watch whoever got you until you respawn. Status effects fill the screen (no text): a grenade or rocket blast turns the edges red, splits the colours and leaves soot spots on the lens; being stunned gives wobbling double vision, a yellow haze and three cartoon stars at the top; being frozen by Frost lets frost creep in from the edges with a blue tint; a flashbang whites the screen out and leaves a fading burnt-in afterimage of what you were looking at. Anyone stunned or dazed has four inked cartoon stars spinning round above their head. Explosions (rockets, grenades, Thumper, fuel drums, cars) have a white flash, a shockwave ring and dust skirt along the ground, a billowing fireball that lights up its surroundings, sparks and debris thrown out, then a dark smoke column. The screen only shakes during a finisher if you're the one being finished. When you're hit, a short thin red arc round the crosshair points to where the shot came from and fades in under a second, turning as you turn.

**Volume** – Sound and Music sliders are in the Options panel (the gear icon, top right of the front menu); they're the same settings as the ones on the pause menu. Both follow a curve, so the low end is very quiet (20% on the slider is about 2% volume).

**Pause menu** – Resume, Controls (every key, grouped), HUD layout and Home, plus settings: **Graphics** (Auto, Low, Med, High), **Frame rate** (30, 60, Max), **Auto weapon switch** (swap to your other gun when the one in hand runs dry; if the other gun is empty too, or you swap back to an empty gun, it reloads automatically; off by default), touch controls on/off, and on phones **Aim assist** (Off, On, Strong), **Aim when firing** (holding Fire also aims down sights) and **Gyro aim**. Sliders: camera sensitivity (up to 6×), **Aim sensitivity (other guns)** (0.1–1), **Sniper scope sensitivity** (0.1–2, HDR and Kar98), sound and music, plus **Battery saver** (30 fps and Low graphics in one tap) and **Show FPS** (a small frame-rate readout). HUD layout switches each panel on or off; **Move & resize** is where everything is placed and sized: drag any panel or control, tap it to make it bigger or smaller (60–160%) and more or less see-through (Opacity 10–100%, kill feed starts at 65%), and on phones an **All buttons** size; the edit bar can be minimised to a strip at the top. Your layout is remembered (phones keep their own layout, separate from desktop) and Reset layout puts it back. Slow motion only kicks in for moments you're part of (your own down, kill or finishing move), never for other players' finishers. XP is only shown on the menu and the end-of-match screen.

**Replays and Home** – clips are recorded from exactly what you see, scope included, at up to 1280×720 and 30 fps (cropped to 16:9, compressed in the background). Lower graphics settings record smaller, slower clips (Medium: 640 wide at 20 fps, 480 at 12 fps on phones; Low: no replays). Only the 4 best of the match are kept: the winning shot first, then finishing moves, headshots and quick double kills. Each clip keeps the moment that decided the fight: the shot that downed someone (from about 3 seconds before it to 1 second after) joined to the last 4.5 seconds before they're finally out (the finish or finishing move) and a moment after, so a long finisher or a downed opponent can't push the actual shot out of the clip. Clips are only shown at the end of the game, behind the video icon on the results bar. Watching a replay pauses the game; Save video downloads it as a video file. Home is a small house icon locked in the top-left corner the whole time you are out of the main menu (in matches, on round and match results and on the pause menu). On phones it's hidden during play so it can't be tapped by accident, and shows at round and match end; Home is also on the pause menu.

**Damage** – every weapon and ability hits 20% harder than it used to (Blaze's fireball 54, Thor's bolt 36, Gorillaz's smash up to 48, Ice Maiden nova 12; Murda Medic heals 48 and Xero reflects 60%), so fights end a little quicker. On top of that, every primary and secondary gun (not fists, the sword, abilities or lethals) does 25% more damage per hit, rounded to whole numbers; the loadout cards show the new figures.

**Reviving** – stand over a downed teammate and they glow green and revive twice as fast (2.5 seconds instead of 5).

**End of match** – "You caught the W!" or "Took the L this time :(" in big letters, the score and four stats: Kills, Damage, Accuracy, Headshots. Rematch and, if any clips were saved, a video icon (with the number of clips on it) that pops up beside it; tap it for the list of clips. Clips are only offered at the end of the game, never between rounds. If you pick nothing within 10 seconds (a small "Home in Ns" countdown shows on the right) it takes you back to the home screen; clicking anywhere on the bar stops the countdown. Home is the permanent house icon.

**Lethals** (what used to be called consumables; press G): Stun Grenade, plus the ones below. Grenade, Stim and HeartBeat have been taken out of the loadout (Simple 1v1s still give everyone grenades).
- **Flash Bang** (2 per round) – thrown like a grenade; anyone who can see the blast gets a white screen for exactly 1 second including the fade-out (0.6 if facing away). Bots lose track of you for as long.
- **Proximity Mine** (2 per round) – laid on the floor in front of you, armed after a second (fast-blinking red light while arming), goes off when an enemy comes within about 1.7 m (60 damage); anyone can shoot it to set it off: a full mine blast with the same damage and radius, counted as the shooter's, so shooting an enemy's mine next to them hurts them, and you take half if you're inside the blast; it's dark grey with a dim red blink, so it's easy to miss. Other blasts set it off too.
- **Cluster Grenade** (2 per round) – a small burst that throws out five bomblets, each a smaller, weaker blast (24 damage) a moment later.
- **Gold Armour** (always on) – you start every round or respawn with 30 armour, which rebuilds back to 30 slowly once you've gone 3 seconds without being hit. Your armour bar (and the sparks when it soaks a hit) is gold instead of blue.

Menu descriptions are short: Stun "Stunned 1s", Flash "Blinded 1s", Mine "Proximity", Cluster "Multi BOOM!!!", Gold Armour "Armour + Rebuilds".

**Character motion** (looks only: aim, speed and hitboxes are unchanged) – legs are driven by where the feet should be: each foot plants and travels back at ground speed, then lifts and swings forward, in whichever direction you're moving (forwards, backwards or sideways), with a pelvis bob, a lean into the run and into starts and stops, a tuck in the air and a knee-bend on landing that grows with the fall. The chest shares the aim pitch through a waist joint (hips go the other way so the head stays over its hitbox), breathes at rest and flinches away from each hit, with the head snapping on headshots. Going down is a short collapse that settles into a prone crawl (arms pulling in turn, legs trailing). Models get a rim light, a contact shadow under the feet, surface relief worked out from their own texture and a small brightness lift for very dark outfits. Per-character extras live in `CHAR_FX` in `index.html` (Wednesday has springy hair, a swinging trouser chain, loose hems and a blink). `CFX` switches each part off for testing.

**Deaths and celebrations** – an ordinary kill drops the body as a ragdoll (it crumples under its own weight, is shoved the way the killing shot or blast was travelling, and folds over or against whatever it lands on) with a few small red blocks round it (no explosion, no slow motion); only finishing moves blow people apart. Downing someone no longer slows the game either. If you finish a downed enemy while every one of their teammates is also down, the whole team is out. Winning a round gets a quick fist pump, a high five between teammates or a little jig, except when a finishing move ends the round: then there's no celebration and the next round starts sooner. Winning the match cuts away from the map to a black throne room: the winners sit down on gold-and-red-velvet thrones under warm spotlights, and each one's kills and damage for the match fade in on the throne back above their head, two blonde ring girls (black one-pieces, red belts, red lips, sparkly silver heels) walk in carrying jewelled crowns and place them on their heads (the crown sits on the top of the head or hair, flames and spikes poke through it), then the camera cuts to a close-up of each winner turning to the lens with a raised eyebrow and a smirk, before going back to the wide shot, which stays behind the results screen. No coloured lights or rings. Space or Enter skips it. Before any celebration or finishing move starts, anyone standing close to a wall, car, prop or other solid object first steps out into clear space (finishers slide both players into open room in under half a second), so nobody clips into or through anything. Round and match results sit in a slim bar across the top so the celebration stays in view. The kill feed sits under the score at 65% opacity.

**Explosive drums** – every Duels map (and any new one) gets a few small clusters of red fuel drums (one to three drums each, spread well apart), tucked beside walls, crates and cars on open ground, clear of spawns, shops, ladders, stairs, water and the train lines. Shoot one and it blows (and sets off its neighbours). They sit in the same spots each time you play a map.

**Bots get everywhere you can on foot** – bots climb ladders, jump and double-jump up onto crates, containers, ledges and roofs (onto a canopy roof like the shop they jump straight up beside the edge and step on once clear), and squeeze through tight gaps beside railings. A jump a bot keeps missing gets dropped from its routes for the rest of the match. Rooftop spawns are only placed where bots can climb to. The only spots they can't reach are ones you need Jet's rocket boost for (cliff-top rocks, the pavilion and signal-box roofs, the crane cab top, the platform shelter roofs).

**Bots under fire** – when a bot gets shot it picks a reaction instead of always walling up: dodging side to side with hops, ducking into cover, backing off, relocating round your side, walling up (less often) or just fighting on. Rushers and breachers mostly fight on.

**Armour plates** – a few glowing blue armour plates sit on the hardest places to get to on each map (high roofs on Street Life, the top of the book tower on Toy Town); Rumble maps have none, since Rumble has no armour. Plates always sit in different areas of the map from each other. Collect one for +50 armour (up to 100); it comes back 60 seconds later somewhere else (away from where it was and from the other plates), and every round start shuffles them to fresh hard-to-reach spots. A plate on a building that gets brought down moves elsewhere after the same 60 seconds. Armour doesn't carry over: everyone starts each round with none.

**Cover** – bullets go through wood (fences, sheds, furniture, wooden floors and crates; each layer takes off 30% of the damage, up to three layers) but not through concrete, brick, walls, cars or built walls. Bodies can't pass through solid structures: anyone pushed into a wall is moved back out.

At 0 HP you go down instead of dying, with 30 downed health that takes full damage, so a few shots finish you: you crawl slowly, and if nobody finishes you, you revive automatically after 5 seconds with whatever downed health you have left, and regen waits 3 seconds before it starts. Downed players can't punch (bots included). While down, clicking fire tags the enemy you're aiming at so they're outlined in red for 3 seconds. In 2v2, if you're down and your teammate is eliminated, you're out too. The opponent can walk up and press F for a finishing move picked at random each time from every finishing move in the game (Pancake comes up twice as often as the others), never the same one twice in a row. Every finishing move has its own sound effects. Knockout, Blur rush, Hot potato and Sky drop end by sending the body flying: over a nearby fence, railing or low wall, or off a roof or ledge (on Sky High, off the skyscraper) whenever one is in reach, otherwise in a random direction until it smashes into the floor or a wall, so you don't know which one you'll get. If the finisher fires or gets shot mid-move the finisher is cancelled and the victim stays down. Every elimination plays the winner's kill effect: each character has two kill effects of their own. XP: +10 per round won, +5 per round lost, 100 XP per level. Built walls sink away after 6 seconds. Walls are easy to shoot down: a hit nudges a brick out along the shot, a broken brick is knocked out, drops and bursts when it hits the ground, any brick left with nothing under it falls too, and knocking out two bricks from the bottom row brings the whole wall down, toppling away from the shots.

The hip-fire crosshair is white and turns red when an enemy is under it, in clear view and within the weapon's range. Aiming down sights with any non-sniper gun switches to first person with see-through ring sights (bigger rings on ARs and LMGs, small on SMGs and pistols, huge on the rocket launcher). Most secondaries have their own sight and zoom: the Uzi an open U-notch with gold dots and little zoom, the Vector an EOTech-style holo box with a cyan ring reticle, the P90 a round smoked-green ring sight with a triangle-in-ring reticle, the UMP a T2-style red-dot tube with the tightest zoom, the Sting no sights at all (the gun cants low and right and a teal chevron shows where you'll hit), the Rocket Launcher a full-screen amber RPG optic with range chevrons, and the Swarm a lock-on display whose brackets snap onto the enemy under your crosshair with pips for the missiles left in the salvo (the MP5 and Dual Pistols keep their rings); bigger guns take longer to raise (0.12s Dual Pistols up to 0.42s Rocket Launcher) and only reach full accuracy once the sights are up. SMGs and pistols hit hardest up close but fall off fast; ARs hit harder per shot but fire slower. The HDR and Kar98 aim through a scope (the HDR's view is a little tighter) and fire on release: hold fire to scope in, let go to shoot (if you let go before the bolt has cycled it fires the moment it's ready); a small red ✕ under the crosshair (or right-click on desktop) cancels without firing, each with a red centre dot and a thin outline in the gun's colour. The shop's Scope adds a see-through red-dot ring sight to the AKM, M4, Fireblade, MP5 and Uzi. Every gun card in the loadout shows a small grey class tag in the top-right corner of its picture (AR, LMG, Sniper, Shotgun, SMG, Pistols, Launcher, Crossbow, Melee). Weapons: pick one primary – HDR sniper (long range, a headshot downs in one, 2 body shots), Kar98 (short-range sniper; white with a black scope so it stands apart from the black HDR), AKM, M4 (gold; harder hits, slower fire) or Fireblade (LMG) – and one secondary – MP5, Uzi (heavier, steadier SMG), Vector, P90, **UMP** (blue long-range SMG with a built-in red dot and see-through mag; 15 damage, about 10 shots a second, 25 rounds, damage holds to about 20 m), **Sting** (teal Skorpion-style machine pistol; the fastest-firing gun at about 28 shots a second, 8 damage, 20 rounds, near-perfect hip fire and the quickest swap), Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher (slow-firing splash), **Swarm** (orange micro-missile launcher: each pull ripples out 4 mini rockets that curve onto whatever you're aiming at, or follow the enemy under your crosshair; 15 splash damage each, 2 salvos then a 3 s reload) or Sword.

**Gun models** – every gun is drawn in a realistic style (`realGun` in `index.html`): real proportions and parts, the gun's own colour as an anodised or cerakote finish on the metal, black polymer or grained walnut furniture, parkerised steel small parts, engraved markings, and no glow or cartoon outline (only sight dots, lenses and LEDs light up). Fifteen are full rebuilds (M4, AKM, FAMAS, Fireblade, M60, HDR, Kar98, SPAS-12, Shotty Horror, MP5, UMP, Sting, Vector, Uzi, Thumper); the Crossbow, P90, Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher, Swarm and Flamethrower keep their shape with the realistic finish. Each model keeps the old bore height, muzzle, sight line and hand positions, so aim, first person and the in-hand pose are unchanged. Lethals, the sword and ninja stars keep the outline in the menu. The M4 is the first gun with the extra finish: polished gold small parts and bolt, a black-chrome barrel, scroll engraving and a gold mag plate; a round counter on the receiver that shows your live ammo in first person (red on the last 5 rounds); and a muzzle that glows hot the more you fire and cools again (first person only; always warm in the loadout picture). The glowing sight dots are only drawn in the loadout picture.

Lethals (press G; for grenades, hold G to see a clean dotted throw arc that stops at the first thing it would hit, with a circle there (the grenade itself still bounces and rolls as before), release to throw toward your crosshair; aiming with Ninja Stars shows their flight path too): Grenade (2 per round, 60 splash damage; thrown a long way, it explodes on anyone it hits, otherwise 2 seconds after it first lands, in a fireball with smoke and a scorch mark), Stim (+45% speed for 6s, 20s cooldown), Stun Grenade (2 per round; anyone caught in it moves at 25% speed for 3 seconds, 1.5 if you catch yourself, but can still shoot) or HeartBeat (see the enemy through walls for 6s, 25s cooldown). The bot gets a random one and uses it too. You also get four buildable walls per round. Magazines are small; reloads are quick, an empty gun in your hands starts reloading by itself after a short beat (even if you switched weapons mid-reload), and R reloads early.

Controls: WASD move · Space jump (press again in mid-air to double jump; it costs 15 stamina) · mouse aim (click to lock) · left click fire (snipers: hold to scope, release to fire) · right click aim down sights / scope · C crouch (toggle; moving forward or jumping stands you up) · E ability · G consumable · T shop grenades (when your consumable isn't the grenade) · V put down a shop Sentry · F finish · R reload · 1/2 or wheel switch · Q build wall · P or Esc pause (controls and sensitivity sliders are on the pause menu). Touch controls switch on automatically on phones and tablets and can be toggled on the pause menu (see **Phones** below).

**Play a friend (online)** – on the front menu, open the **Play a friend** icon (top right); **Host game** gives you a 4-letter room code; your friend opens the game, types the code and presses **Join**. The friend's character and guns are whatever they pick on their menu; the host chooses the mode and map and presses Start, and the friend takes the place of the first enemy bot (in 1v1 you fight each other; in 2v2 and Free for all the other slots stay bots). The host's game runs the match; the friend's movement and shots show instantly on their screen and the host decides the hits. If the friend leaves or drops out, a bot takes over their fighter and they can rejoin with the same code. Connections go device to device (WebRTC via the free PeerJS matchmaking server); a few strict mobile or work networks may block the direct link.

**Secondaries** are the MP5, Uzi, Vector, P90, Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher and Sword (the Thumper and Ninja Stars are out of the loadout and the bots' kits).

**Vector** – the fastest-firing gun in the game (about 25 rounds a second, 14 damage a bullet, 25-round mag, so it empties in about a second); almost no kick but the spread opens up fast if you hold the trigger. Hot-pink "Super V" lower under a flat-top gunmetal upper with a full rail, flip sights, weapon light, slotted shroud and brake, and a folding skeleton stock.

**P90** – 50-round bullpup (15 damage a bullet) with hip-fire almost as tight as aiming; slower to aim and a 1.3 s reload. Sculpted charcoal body with neon green trim, trigger and thumbhole cut-outs, a smoked see-through top mag showing the rounds lying crossways and an orange follower, a rounded ring sight and a flash hider.

**Shotty Horror** (replaces the AA-12) – a side-by-side double-barrelled shotgun (break action, top lever, twin gold triggers, cyan stock with a pink glow strip, gold bead sight): two shells, fired one after the other as fast as you can pull the trigger (0.26 s apart), then a 1.2 s reload. Each shell is 10 pellets of 13 damage (130 if they all land within 7 m; damage drops off to 45% by 20 m). It's a two-tap gun: a single shell tops out at 75% of their health, so it takes both barrels to down someone; only an absolutely point-blank shell (within 1.5 m, at least half the pellets hitting) downs in one.

**Phones** – landscape only (a "Turn your phone sideways" screen shows in portrait and pauses the match). Move stick on the left, drag anywhere to look (fast swipes turn further), and a thumb-reach arc of buttons round Fire: Aim, Jump, Crouch, Reload, Swap, Ability, Wall and Item, with Finish and Nade appearing only when you can use them; counts and cooldowns show on the buttons. Add the game to your home screen for full screen (Android also goes full screen and locks to landscape when a game starts; iPhone shows a one-time Add to Home Screen tip). Sounds, icons and three.js are cached for quick loading. Phones start on Medium graphics with a 60 fps cap; menus, pause and results screens drop to 20 fps to keep the phone cool. Small props aren't drawn far away on Medium and Low, and characters are built from about half as many pieces as before. Auto starts recent high-end phone GPUs on High, and after it has had to drop a level it tries one level higher again next time. Switching away from the app pauses the match. On phone menus the Start button floats in the corner.

**Parkour** – 12 obstacle courses with double jump, swings and moving platforms (switched off in the menu for now; the code is still there).

Static scenery is merged into a handful of meshes at load (instead of ~1,000 separate boxes, each with its own outline), which keeps the frame rate up on Maple Street.

Under the hood the match runs on a fixed 60-ticks-a-second clock on every device, player input reaches the game as one command per tick, shot spread uses a per-match seed, and the match state can be saved as a small snapshot: groundwork for online multiplayer.

It's a single static `index.html` (three.js and fonts load from CDNs), served by GitHub Pages. To play locally, open `index.html` in a browser.
