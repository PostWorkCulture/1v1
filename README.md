# imok 1v1

A blocky three.js shooter you play in the browser against bots: 1v1, 2v2 or free for all.

**Duels** – pick **1v1**, **2v2** (you and a bot teammate against two bots; teammates spawn side by side (your teammate stands to your left with a gap between you, facing the same way, so you can see each other), can't hurt each other, and once you're out you spectate your teammate) or **Free for all** (you against three bots, everyone for themselves). 1v1 and 2v2 are first to five rounds; Free for all is a deathmatch with no rounds: everyone respawns 3 seconds after going out, at the spawn point furthest from the others, and the first to 10 kills wins (10-minute limit, most kills wins if time runs out). Every map has a shop at each end. Maps: Maple Street, Wreck Cove and Rail Yard (Sky Islands is coming soon and shows as a locked tile in the menu). On Maple Street every house can be entered; most have two floors (each staircase ends on a landing with a wide, open side onto the floor, so you step straight off at the top), balconies, lofts with rooftop terraces, and big upstairs windows you can walk out of. Ledges and air-con units under the side windows let you climb onto the roofs, and every pitched roof can be walked on. The two big end houses also have a hop-up (a bin and two crates) against one side that takes you up onto the window ledge and in through an upstairs window. Rooms are tall (about 4 m downstairs, 3.6 m upstairs and in the lofts), so the houses don't feel cramped. The street sits on a square plot with six houses: the four big ones have yellow, green, red and blue roofs so you can call out positions, and the two flat-roofed ones have white and light grey roof decks. There's a shop at each end of the road, and the road has a white dashed centre line. Rockets, grenades and fireballs blow up the cars (a new one is parked back in its spot later). The flat roofs have wooden fence panels and crates to fight behind: bullets go through them and they break apart. Inside the houses the wooden floors, stairs and the wooden partition wall can be shot through but the concrete outer walls can't, and short wooden furniture gets chipped down by gunfire but still works as cover. Each big house has its own character: the yellow one is **Maple Motors**, a garage with a car on the bay floor with its bonnet up, tyre stacks, oil drums, a workbench and tools; the red one is a **clinic** with a reception desk, waiting chairs, a ward with beds, drips and a heart monitor, an ambulance at the kerb and a helipad on the loft terrace; the green one is a family **home** with a chimney (smoke curls out of it), a fire in the hearth, a kitchen, dining table, sofa and rug; and the blue one is **Maple Mart**, a corner shop with shelves of groceries, a till, drinks fridges, trolleys out front and a stockroom of boxes. Small loose things (cans, bottles, cones, chairs, drips, lamps, mugs, the till...) get knocked over by bullets, blasts and people walking into them, and stay down until the next round. Every solid piece of furniture takes damage: beds, counters, shelves and fridges get chipped down, tyre stacks, drums and boxes blow apart, and enough bullets will set a car off. The two flat-roofed houses can be brought down: the first rocket, grenade or fireball that hits one blows the roof wall off and leaves it cracked, leaning and smoking; a second one makes it slowly sink and fold into a pile of big blocks you can climb over. Everything is rebuilt at the start of the next round. Parked cars (with spinning hubcaps that blur at speed and kick up a little dust) pull out now and then and drive along the street at their own speed (they stop for players and walls), birds fly over, clouds drift, trees sway and butterflies flutter round the front gardens. **Rail Yard** is a freight yard with two live lines through the middle: a freight train comes out of the tunnel at one end every half a minute or so on each line (in opposite directions), blows its horn and runs the length of the yard, so the middle keeps closing and opening. You can ride on top of the wagons (or inside a hopper or on a flatcar), and anyone standing in front of a train is flung clear and badly hurt. Signals turn red while a train is in the section and the level crossing lights flash and the barriers drop as one approaches. Around the tracks: a loading dock and a brick goods shed with a mezzanine and a roof hatch, an engine shed with a parked locomotive and roof stairs, a signal box with a lever frame upstairs, a gantry crane straddling all four tracks (stairs up to a 16 m walkway), a water tower (11 m deck), a footbridge over the line (drop onto passing trains), parked wagons on the sidings (walk into the open box wagon, climb onto the tank and flatcar), and stepped container stacks you climb with crates. Two site cabins crack under one blast and collapse into rubble under a second, red fuel drums explode when shot (and set each other off), crates break, pallets, sleepers and cable reels get chipped, and cones and buckets get knocked over. **Wreck Cove** is a sunny cove closed in by cliffs and headlands. A galleon lies broken in two on the beach: both halves have a lower gun deck, a main deck and stairs, the stern has the captain's cabin under a high castle, the bow a raised forecastle, and the fallen mainmast bridges the two. Around it: a thatched Tiki Bar (crates up onto the roof), a lifeguard tower, a row of beach huts, a rock lookout up the cliff, a volleyball court, dunes, rocks, umbrellas and a bonfire. A jetty runs out to a moored fishing boat, and there's a swim raft, a swan pedalo and rocks in the shallows. The sea is the star: a swell rolls in every few seconds, rises over the shallows and breaks into a white curl and a foaming bore with spray, then rushes up the sand as a thin sheet edged with bubbly foam and slides back leaving lace and dark wet sand. Foam swirls in the shallows, sunlight ripples over the sand under the water, and the surface glitters in the sun (phones get a lighter version). You can wade out to the buoy line, a little slower: every step splashes, landing in the water makes a bigger splash, and bullets, thrown items and explosions all splash where they hit the water. Sailing boats, a yacht, a speedboat and a cargo ship pass offshore, gulls circle and crabs scuttle along the waterline. Each round you and the bot start at spawn points far apart: out in the street, up on roofs and upper floors, or inside houses. The bot climbs stairs and roofs too.

**Bot play styles** – every round each bot secretly picks one of ten styles (and weapons to match), so you have to work out what you're up against:
- **Rusher** – charges straight at you, jukes and jumps, never retreats.
- **Sniper** – takes high ground far away, scopes in, relocates after every couple of shots, switches to its sidearm if you get close.
- **Lurker** – hides out of sight near you and springs when you come close or turn your back.
- **Flanker** – circles round out of view and hits you from the side.
- **Turtle** – walls up when it sees you, peeks from cover, backs off when hurt.
- **Duelist** – strafes and trades shots at mid range.
- **Hunter** – always has a rough fix on where you are and keeps tracking you down.
- **Breacher** – shotgun; hops between hiding spots, each one closer, then bursts in.
- **Kiter** – holds its favourite range: backs off as you push, follows as you run.
- **Camper** – picks one spot with a view, walls up and holds the angle all round.

All styles push when you're low, play safer when hurt, and chase the clock late in a round when behind on health. Sniper, Turtle, Kiter and Camper aren't used in Rumble.

**Rumble** – Duels with no guns (also 1v1, 2v2 or free for all): fists only (18 per punch), on the small Backyard map or The House. Punch someone while you're falling for 20 damage: you both get knocked back and they're stunned for a second. Abilities, walls, pick-ups, slams and finishing moves all still work.

The menu is a full-screen, three-step setup: 1) mode and map, 2) character (with a profile preview; Blaze is selected by default) and consumable, 3) primary and secondary weapons (with previews and stats). Start turns green once everything is picked.

The menu opens with everything pre-picked so you can hit Start straight away: the top map, Blaze (top left of the character grid), HDR, MP5 and Grenade.

**Shop** – every map has an open SHOP kiosk on the clearest ground near the middle, reachable from all sides; the edges of its roof glow so you can spot it, and nothing round it gets in your way (just one slim post in the middle). Walk up to it and a quick menu pops up: press a number (or tap) to take one item – **Health** (+50 HP), **Armour** (blocks the next 50 damage), or **Grenades** (3 more; if your consumable is the grenade they simply add to your G count, otherwise they're thrown with T). In Rumble it's Health, Armour or **Speed** (+25% for 30 s). After a pick the shop restocks for you in 30 seconds. The fight doesn't pause while you're there. Unused shop items carry into the next round only if you (or your team) won the round; lose or draw and they're gone.

Characters (12): Brute (a slate-skinned giant with rage veins; 130 HP, leaps and smashes the ground for up to 48 damage and knockback, breaking nearby walls), Jet (an ace pilot with a chrome jetpack; flight), Blaze (a fire brawler with flame hair; fireballs), Blink (a street magician; teleport dash), Tank (a mech pilot in a yellow exo-frame; 150 HP and a shield), Ghost (a hooded spectre; invisibility), Frost (an ice mage; frost nova that slows anyone within 9 m), Patch (a field medic; heals 48 HP), Volt (an electric punk with tesla coils; 36-damage lightning bolt), Hook (a climber; grapples to wherever you aim), Zip (a sprinter; fastest runner, 90 HP, sprint burst) and Thorn (a bark-armoured warden; blocks 30% and reflects 60% of the damage taken), each with an ability on E.

**Stamina and regen** – hold Shift to run (about 40% faster; on touch, push the move stick all the way out). Running drains stamina, and so does aiming down sights (a bit more slowly). The bar refills a second after you stop; when it hits 0 you move 25% slower and can't run until it's back to a quarter full. Getting shot drains stamina too, more for harder hits (an HDR body shot takes most of it, an SMG bullet a sliver). Health regenerates once you've gone 5 seconds without being hit: 6 HP a second up to half health, then 3 HP a second; bots regenerate too.

**HUD** – everyone moves 25% faster than before. Bottom left is a vitals panel with health (green), armour (blue) and stamina (purple) bars in that order, and above it a compact items row: an icon, its key and how many you have (ability, consumable, walls, plus shop grenades and speed boots when you have them). Top left is a small leaderboard of everyone in the match with kills, assists (damaged someone a teammate or rival then eliminated within 15 s) and deaths; anyone out for the current round is greyed. Top right is a minimap that turns with you and shows the map layout, the shop (gold square) and teammates (blue). Enemies never show unless they fire: each shot leaves a tiny red dot where they were, fading over a couple of seconds. Whenever anyone is eliminated a small red X marks the spot for 3 seconds. While you're out, the vitals and weapon panels switch to the player you're watching (name, kills/assists/deaths, live health, armour, stamina, gun and ammo); in deathmatch you watch whoever got you until you respawn. Being stunned puts a black-and-yellow zigzag round the edge of the screen, being frozen by Frost a blue-and-white one, and taking a grenade or rocket blast a black-and-red one (no text).

**Pause menu** – Resume, Controls (every key, grouped), HUD layout and Home, plus touch controls and the two sensitivity sliders (aim/scope sensitivity runs 0.1–1, default 0.30). HUD layout lets you switch any panel (score, leaderboard, minimap, kill feed, vitals, items, weapon and ammo, controls hint, name tags) on or off and make each one bigger or smaller (60–160%). Move panels lets you drag every panel to wherever you want it on screen (positions scale with the window); your layout is remembered, and Reset puts it back. Slow motion only kicks in for moments you're part of (your own down, kill or finishing move), never for other players' finishers. XP is only shown on the menu and the end-of-match screen.

**Replays and Home** – clips are recorded from exactly what you see, scope included, at up to 1280×720 and 30 fps (cropped to 16:9, compressed in the background). Only the 4 best of the match are kept: the winning shot first, then finishing moves, headshots and quick double kills. Watching a replay pauses the game; Save video downloads it as a video file. Home is a small house icon that stays on screen under the minimap the whole match (it's also on the pause menu).

**Damage** – every weapon and ability hits 20% harder than it used to (Blaze's fireball 54, Volt's bolt 36, Brute's smash up to 48, Frost nova 12; Patch heals 48 and Thorn reflects 60%), so fights end a little quicker.

**Reviving** – stand over a downed teammate and they glow green and revive twice as fast (2 seconds instead of 4).

**End of match** – "You caught the W!" or "Took the L this time :(" in big letters, the score and four stats: Kills, Damage, Accuracy, Headshots. Rematch and View Clips (every replay from the match); Home is the permanent house icon.

**Lethals** (what used to be called consumables; press G): Grenade, Stim, Stun Grenade and Sense Pack as before, plus:
- **Flash Bang** (2 per round) – thrown like a grenade; anyone who can see the blast gets a near-white screen for 2 seconds (1 second if facing away). Bots lose track of you for as long.
- **Proximity Mine** (2 per round) – laid on the floor in front of you, armed after a second (fast-blinking red light while arming), goes off when an enemy comes within about 1.7 m (60 damage); it's dark grey with a dim red blink, so it's easy to miss. Other blasts set it off too.
- **Cluster Grenade** (2 per round) – a small burst that throws out five bomblets, each a smaller, weaker blast (24 damage) a moment later.
- **Golden Armour** (always on) – you start every round or respawn with 50 armour, which rebuilds back to 50 at a quarter of the speed health regenerates.

**Deaths and celebrations** – an ordinary kill drops the body flat on the ground with a few small red blocks round it (no explosion, no slow motion); only finishing moves blow people apart. Downing someone no longer slows the game either. If you finish a downed enemy while every one of their teammates is also down, the whole team is out. Winning a round gets a quick fist pump, a high five between teammates or a little jig. The big show is saved for winning the match: the winners stay on the ground, face the camera and do silly dances (chicken, robot, sprinkler, twist, disco point, floss, wave) in one of four light shows (Rave, Disco, Spotlight or Neon) with glowing fireworks overhead; the camera holds still, front-on. Before any celebration or finishing move starts, anyone standing close to a wall, car, prop or other solid object first steps out into clear space (finishers slide both players into open room in under half a second), so nobody clips into or through anything. Round and match results sit in a slim bar across the top so the celebration stays in view. The kill feed sits under the score at 65% opacity.

**Bots under fire** – when a bot gets shot it picks a reaction instead of always walling up: dodging side to side with hops, ducking into cover, backing off, relocating round your side, walling up (less often) or just fighting on. Rushers and breachers mostly fight on.

**Armour plates** – a few glowing blue armour plates sit on the hardest places to get to on each map (high roofs on Maple Street, the upstairs of The House, the shed roof in the Backyard). Grab one for +50 armour (up to 100); it comes back 30 seconds later and at every round start.

**Cover** – bullets go through wood (fences, sheds, furniture, wooden floors and crates; each layer takes off 30% of the damage, up to three layers) but not through concrete, brick, walls, cars or built walls. Bodies can't pass through solid structures: throws and slams stop at walls, a Home run finisher stops at the first wall it hits, and anyone pushed into a wall is moved back out.

At 0 HP you go down instead of dying, with 30 downed health that takes full damage, so a few shots finish you: you crawl slowly, and if nobody finishes you, you revive automatically after 4 seconds with whatever downed health you have left, and regen waits 5 seconds before it starts. While down, clicking fire tags the enemy you're aiming at so they're outlined in red for 3 seconds. In 2v2, if you're down and your teammate is eliminated, you're out too. The opponent can walk up and press F for their character's signature finishing move: Brute Pancake, Frost Ice sculpture, Jet Sky drop, Blaze Roast, Blink Sawn in half, Tank Knockout, Ghost Heartbreaker, Patch Clear!, Volt Overcharge, Hook Hot potato, Zip Blur rush, Thorn Dog pile (Gone fishing, Piledriver, Shadow grab and Overgrown are built but saved for later). If the finisher fires or gets shot mid-move the finisher is cancelled and the victim stays down. Every elimination plays the winner's kill effect: each character has two kill effects of their own. XP: +10 per round won, +5 per round lost, 100 XP per level. Built walls sink away after 6 seconds, break apart brick by brick, and gunfire knocks them down twice as fast as before.

The hip-fire crosshair is white and turns red when an enemy is under it, in clear view and within the weapon's range. Aiming down sights with any non-sniper gun switches to first person with see-through ring sights (bigger rings on ARs and LMGs, small on SMGs and pistols, huge on the rocket launcher); bigger guns take longer to raise (0.12s Dual Pistols up to 0.42s Rocket Launcher) and only reach full accuracy once the sights are up. SMGs and pistols hit hardest up close but fall off fast; ARs hit harder per shot but fire slower. The HDR and Kar98 aim through a scope (the HDR's view is a little tighter), each with a red centre dot and a thin outline in the gun's colour. The shop's Scope adds a see-through red-dot ring sight to the AKM, M4, LMG, MP5 and Uzi. Weapons: pick one primary – HDR sniper (long range, a headshot downs in one, 2 body shots), Kar98 (short-range sniper), AKM, M4 (gold; harder hits, slower fire) or LMG – and one secondary – MP5, Uzi (heavier, steadier SMG), Rocket Launcher (slow-firing splash), Ninja Stars or Sword.

Lethals (press G; for grenades, hold G to see a clean dotted throw arc that stops at the first thing it would hit, with a circle there (the grenade itself still bounces and rolls as before), release to throw toward your crosshair; aiming with Ninja Stars shows their flight path too): Grenade (2 per round, 60 splash damage; thrown a long way, it explodes on anyone it hits, otherwise 2 seconds after it first lands, in a fireball with smoke and a scorch mark), Stim (+45% speed for 5s, 20s cooldown), Stun Grenade (2 per round, stuns anyone caught in the flash) or Sense Pack (see the enemy through walls for 6s, 25s cooldown). The bot gets a random one and uses it too. You also get four buildable walls per round. Magazines are small; reloads are quick, an empty gun in your hands starts reloading by itself after a short beat (even if you switched weapons mid-reload), and R reloads early.

Controls: WASD move · Shift run · Space jump (press again in mid-air to double jump when stamina is above half; it costs 15 stamina) · mouse aim (click to lock) · left click fire · right click aim down sights / scope · C crouch (toggle; running stands you up) / pick up a downed opponent · E ability · G consumable · T shop grenades (when your consumable isn't the grenade) · F finish · R reload · 1/2 or wheel switch · Q build wall · P or Esc pause (controls and sensitivity sliders are on the pause menu). Touch controls (move stick, drag to look, on-screen buttons) switch on automatically on phones and tablets and can be toggled on the pause menu.

**Parkour** – 12 obstacle courses with double jump, swings and moving platforms.

Static scenery is merged into a handful of meshes at load (instead of ~1,000 separate boxes, each with its own outline), which keeps the frame rate up on Maple Street.

It's a single static `index.html` (three.js and fonts load from CDNs), served by GitHub Pages. To play locally, open `index.html` in a browser.
