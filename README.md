# imok 1v1

A blocky three.js shooter you play in the browser against bots: 1v1, 2v2 or free for all.

## Publishing rules (read before changing anything)

The site is published by the **Check and publish** GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`). Every push to `main` is checked first; if the check fails nothing is published and the last good version stays live.

- **Run the check before every commit:** `node tools/check.js` (syntax of every script in `index.html` and `sw.js`). `node tools/check.js --smoke` also loads the page in headless Chromium and starts a Duels match (needs Playwright). The pre-commit hook runs the syntax check for you once it's switched on for the clone: `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`.
- **Never use `//` comments in `index.html`.** The code is written in long single lines, so a `//` silently turns everything after it on that line into a comment (this took the site down on 30 Oct 2026). Use `/* ... */`. The check flags a `//` with code after it.
- **Work in one place, on `main`.** Make changes in the linked local 1v1 folder (or a clone of this repo), commit to `main`, and push before finishing. Don't leave work on side branches or only as patch files. If a session can't push, it must say so plainly.
- **Pull before you start** (`git pull`), since several sessions work on this repo.
- **Read `DESIGN.md` first.** It's the design memory: the rules in force, things never to add back, and a daily log. Update it when a design decision changes.

**Menu look** – monochrome, copied from the Unity version: black cards with thin grey edges, white type, plain Arial lettering, and whatever is picked shown in white (a solid white option in the segmented bars and tabs, a white edge on map buttons, character tiles and weapon cards; no neon glows). Colour is used only as an occasional highlight: the red-orange gradient on the one main button of each screen (Character / Loadout / Start, Resume), the red "1v1" in the logo and the red underline under the map name. The map picture has a white edge, a white tag chip, the name in big plain type, the red underline and the one-liner in a dark box. The character stage is lit in plain white light (the characters keep their own colours). The pause, settings, results, shop and replay panels use the same look, with white sliders.

**Loading picture** – the map picture behind the map's name as a match loads is shot fresh at your screen's own size (up to 2560 px wide) from the map's hero camera, so it's sharp rather than a stretched menu picture.

**Colours** – menus, pause/settings, the results screen and HUD panels use navy blue instead of black (every near-black background, border and shade in the stylesheets was shifted to the same navy hue at a similar darkness; text colours untouched). tools/navy-theme.py is the converter, so anything new added in black can be run through it. Every red in the menus (home menu, settings, results, shop, replays: Character/Start/Rematch buttons, the logo's "1v1", Defeat, highlights) is neon pink instead; in-game reds (enemy score, damage, Man Down, countdown) stay red. tools/pink-menus.py does that conversion.

**Duels** – pick **2v2** (you and a bot teammate against two bots, your teammate playing at the same bot level as the enemies; teammates spawn side by side, and the two teams start somewhere neither can see the other, so a round never opens with both teams staring at each other in the open (your teammate stands to your left with a gap between you, facing the same way, so you can see each other), can't hurt each other, and once you're out you spectate your teammate) or **Free for all** (you against three bots, everyone for themselves). 1v1 and 2v2 are first to five rounds; Free for all is a deathmatch with no rounds: everyone respawns 3 seconds after going out, at the spawn point furthest from the others, and it's timed: pick a match length of 2, 4 or 6 minutes under the free-for-all size (4 by default), and whoever has the most kills when the clock runs out wins (ties go to whoever died less). **Bot play styles** – every round each bot secretly picks one of ten styles (and weapons to match), so you have to work out what you're up against:
- **Rusher** – charges straight at you, jukes and jumps, never retreats.
- **Sniper** – takes high ground far away, scopes in, relocates after every couple of shots, switches to its sidearm if you get close.
- **Lurker** – hides out of sight near you and springs when you come close or turn your back.
- **Flanker** – circles round out of view and hits you from the side.
- **Turtle** – walls up when it sees you, peeks from cover, backs off when hurt.
- **Duelist** – strafes and trades shots at mid range.
- **Hunter** – always has a rough fix on where you are and keeps tracking you down.
- **Breacher** – shotgun; hops between hiding spots, each one closer, then bursts in.
- **Kiter** – holds its favourite range: backs off as you push, follows as you run.
- **Camper** – picks one spot with a view, walls up and holds the angle all round.

**Bot tactics** – on top of their style, bots now use cover and peeks (at range they duck behind something close, step back out to fire a few shots, then duck back in to reload), pre-fire corners (when you've just gone out of sight nearby they keep their gun on where you were and fire as it comes into view), jump shots in mid-range gunfights, smarter throwables (grenades go into a group of enemies first, then wherever you've just ducked out of sight; flashes and stuns are thrown before a rush), mines laid on chokepoints (ladder ends, doorways and gaps under 2 m wide, steps) instead of at their feet, and snipers that prefer rooftops. How often depends on difficulty. All styles push when you're low, play safer when hurt, and chase the clock late in a round when behind on health. Sniper, Turtle, Kiter and Camper aren't used in Rumble. A bot you kill comes back hunting you until it next gets a kill, but the hunt doesn't blind it: if another player shoots it, or is in plain sight within about 20 m, it deals with them first and then carries on after you (and any bot turns on whoever has hit it in the last few seconds).

**Character motion** (looks only: aim, speed and hitboxes are unchanged) – legs are driven by where the feet should be: each foot plants and travels back at ground speed, then lifts and swings forward, in whichever direction you're moving (forwards, backwards or sideways), with a pelvis bob, a lean into the run and into starts and stops, a tuck in the air and a knee-bend on landing that grows with the fall. The chest shares the aim pitch through a waist joint (hips go the other way so the head stays over its hitbox), breathes at rest and flinches away from each hit, with the head snapping on headshots. Feet are planted at a point in the world while they are down, so they don't slide through turns, stops or changes of speed; each swing leaves and lands near ground speed; after a stop or a turn on the spot a foot that is out of place takes a small step back under the hip; knees ease into full stretch; the stride's shape follows a smoothed speed so running into a wall doesn't snap the pose; other fighters' bodies turn towards their aim over a few frames. The game simulates 60 times a second, and drawn positions (every body, and your camera with yours) are blended between steps so movement is even on 90, 120 and 144 Hz screens. Going down is a short collapse that settles into a prone crawl (arms pulling in turn, legs trailing). Shots at a downed player count if they hit the fixed downed box or the body you can actually see (head, chest, hips, knees and feet), so the collapse, a raised head or a body turned away from a wall can't make hits miss. Models get a rim light, a contact shadow under the feet, surface relief worked out from their own texture and a small brightness lift for very dark outfits. Per-character extras live in `CHAR_FX` in `index.html` (Wednesday has springy hair, a swinging trouser chain, loose hems and a blink). `CFX` switches each part off for testing.

**Deaths and celebrations** – an ordinary kill drops the body as a ragdoll (it crumples under its own weight, is shoved the way the killing shot or blast was travelling, and folds over or against whatever it lands on) with a few small red blocks round it (no explosion, no slow motion). Downing someone no longer slows the game either. If you finish a downed enemy while every one of their teammates is also down, the whole team is out. Winning a round gets a quick fist pump, a high five between teammates or a little jig. Winning the match, in every mode including Free for all, shows the Victory banner for about a second and a half and then goes straight to the results scoreboard (the throne-room crown scene has been removed). Before any celebration starts, anyone standing close to a wall, car, prop or other solid object first steps out into clear space, so nobody clips into or through anything. Round and match results sit in a slim bar across the top so the celebration stays in view. The kill feed sits under the score at 65% opacity.

**Explosive drums** – every Duels map (and any new one) gets a few small clusters of red fuel drums (one to three drums each, spread well apart), tucked beside walls, crates and cars on open ground, clear of spawns, shops, ladders, stairs, water and the train lines. Shoot one and it blows (and sets off its neighbours). They sit in the same spots each time you play a map.

**Bots get everywhere you can on foot** – bots climb ladders, jump and double-jump up onto crates, containers, ledges and roofs (onto a canopy roof like the shop they jump straight up beside the edge and step on once clear), and squeeze through tight gaps beside railings. A jump a bot keeps missing gets dropped from its routes for the rest of the match. Rooftop spawns are only placed where bots can climb to.

**Bots pick the right gun for the range** – bots swap to whichever of their two guns suits the distance: shotguns, SMGs and other close-range guns when you're up close, rifles and snipers further out (never a rocket at point blank, and they think twice before swapping to an empty gun). How quickly and how reliably they do it depends on difficulty: Easy bots rethink every few seconds and often stay on the wrong gun, Normal ones every second or two, and Hard and Extreme ones swap almost as soon as the range changes.

**Bots under fire** – when a bot gets shot it picks a reaction instead of always walling up: dodging side to side with hops, ducking into cover, backing off, relocating round your side, walling up (less often) or just fighting on. Rushers and breachers mostly fight on.

**Cover** – bullets go through wood (fences, sheds, furniture, wooden floors and crates; each layer takes off 30% of the damage, up to three layers) but not through concrete, brick, walls or cars. Bodies can't pass through solid structures: anyone pushed into a wall is moved back out.

At 0 HP you go down instead of dying, with 30 downed health that takes full damage, so a few shots finish you: you crawl slowly, and if nobody finishes you, you revive automatically after 5 seconds with whatever downed health you have left, and regen waits 3 seconds before it starts. Downed players can't punch (bots included). While down, clicking fire tags the enemy you're aiming at so they're outlined in red for 3 seconds. In 2v2, if you're down and your teammate is eliminated, you're out too. Every elimination plays the winner's kill effect: each character has two kill effects of their own. XP: +10 per round won, +5 per round lost, 100 XP per level.  Walls are easy to shoot down: a hit nudges a brick out along the shot, a broken brick is knocked out, drops and bursts when it hits the ground, any brick left with nothing under it falls too, and knocking out two bricks from the bottom row brings the whole wall down, toppling away from the shots.

**Phones** – landscape only (a "Turn your phone sideways" screen shows in portrait and pauses the match).
