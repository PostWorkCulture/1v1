# imok 1v1

A blocky three.js shooter you play in the browser against bots: 1v1, 2v2 or free for all.

## Publishing rules (read before changing anything)

The site is published by the **Check and publish** GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`). Every push to `main` is checked first; if the check fails nothing is published and the last good version stays live.

- **Run the check before every commit:** `node tools/check.js` (syntax of every script in `index.html` and `sw.js`). `node tools/check.js --smoke` also loads the page in headless Chromium and starts a Duels match (needs Playwright). The pre-commit hook runs the syntax check for you once it's switched on for the clone: `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`.
- **Never use `//` comments in `index.html`.** The code is written in long single lines, so a `//` silently turns everything after it on that line into a comment (this took the site down on 30 Oct 2026). Use `/* ... */`. The check flags a `//` with code after it.
- **Work in one place, on `main`.** Make changes in the linked local 1v1 folder (or a clone of this repo), commit to `main`, and push before finishing. Don't leave work on side branches or only as patch files. If a session can't push, it must say so plainly.
- **Pull before you start** (`git pull`), since several sessions work on this repo.

**Menu look** – monochrome, copied from the Unity version: black cards with thin grey edges, white type, plain Arial lettering, and whatever is picked shown in white (a solid white option in the segmented bars and tabs, a white edge on map buttons, character tiles and weapon cards; no neon glows). **Duels** – pick **2v2** (you and a bot teammate against two bots, your teammate playing at the same bot level as the enemies; teammates spawn side by side, and the two teams start somewhere neither can see the other, so a round never opens with both teams staring at each other in the open (your teammate stands to your left with a gap between you, facing the same way, so you can see each other), can't hurt each other, and once you're out you spectate your teammate) or **Free for all** (you against three bots, everyone for themselves). 1v1 and 2v2 are first to five rounds; Free for all is a deathmatch with no rounds: everyone respawns 3 seconds after going out, at the spawn point furthest from the others, and it's timed: pick a match length of 3, 5 or 10 minutes under the free-for-all size (5 by default), and whoever has the most kills when the clock runs out wins (ties go to whoever died less). **Bot play styles** – every round each bot secretly picks one of ten styles (and weapons to match), so you have to work out what you're up against:
- **Rusher** – charges straight at you, jukes and jumps, never retreats.
- **Sniper** – takes high ground far away, scopes in, relocates after every couple of shots, switches to its sidearm if you get close.
- **Lurker** – hides out of sight near you and springs when you come close or turn your back.
- **Flanker** – circles round out of view and hits you from the side.
- **Turtle** – walls up when it sees you, peeks from cover, backs off when hurt.
- **Duelist** – strafes and trades shots at mid range.
- **Hunter** – always has a rough fix on where you are and keeps tracking you down.
- **Breacher** – shotgun; hops between hiding spots, each one closer, then bursts in.
- **Kiter** – holds its favourite range: backs off as you push, follows as you run.
- **Camper** – picks one spot with a view, walls up and holds the angle all round.

**Bot tactics** – on top of their style, bots now use cover and peeks (at range they duck behind something close, step back out to fire a few shots, then duck back in to reload), pre-fire corners (when you've just gone out of sight nearby they keep their gun on where you were and fire as it comes into view), jump shots in mid-range gunfights, smarter throwables (grenades go into a group of enemies first, then wherever you've just ducked out of sight; flashes and stuns are thrown before a rush), mines laid on chokepoints (ladder ends, doorways and gaps under 2 m wide, steps) instead of at their feet, and snipers that prefer rooftops. How often depends on difficulty. In Snipers only every bot plays for angles: each one is a sniper or a camper, they perch on high spots and peek from cover (step out, take the shot, duck back), back off into hiding when hit instead of dodging or charging, and walk between angles rather than sprinting about.

All styles push when you're low, play safer when hurt, and chase the clock late in a round when behind on health. Sniper, Turtle, Kiter and Camper aren't used in Rumble. A bot you kill comes back hunting you until it next gets a kill, but the hunt doesn't blind it: if another player shoots it, or is in plain sight within about 20 m, it deals with them first and then carries on after you (and any bot turns on whoever has hit it in the last few seconds).

Characters (11), each a sculpted 3D model: Gorillaz (a cyber gorilla mech), Jet (an astronaut ace with a jetpack on his back; the back of his helmet is filled in so you don't see inside it from above), Starscreen (a hooded horned spectre), Murda Medic (a field medic), Crusty (a clown with purple hair and a green suit), Wednesday (a goth runner), Anti Hero (a masked caped hero), Dracz (a vampire), Spacebot (an astronaut robot), Lil Pink and Shadow (an all-black tactical operative in a Sentinel robot mask with a red visor slit and red-striped forearm guards; built in Blender from the humanoid template rather than Tripo, baked with tools/models/bake.mjs using the cuts in tools/models/cuts/roboid.json, menu face pictures from tools/models/cards_k.mjs; the only fighter with realistic body proportions: he's scaled to the cast's height (about 2.9 m in game units, eyes a little above everyone's), stays slimmer than the blocky fighters; he walks with the same procedural six-part gait as everyone else (STRIDE in index.html can lengthen a fighter's steps, unused for now)). **Stamina and regen** – there's no sprinting: everyone walks 25% faster than the original walk speed instead (bots too). Walking drains stamina slowly and aiming down sights faster; stand still and it starts refilling after half a second, full again in about 3 seconds (twice as fast with the shop's Stamina). At 0 you move 25% slower until it's back to a quarter. Getting shot drains stamina too, more for harder hits (an HDR body shot takes most of it, an SMG bullet a sliver). A double jump costs 15 stamina and works whenever you have that much. Health regenerates once you've gone 3 seconds without being hit, at 10 HP a second; bots regenerate too.

**In-game look (monochrome, matching the menus)** – every HUD panel is black with a thin border: the score bar shows the round, the timer and five pips under each score (yours white, theirs red; deathmatch shows the kill target instead), the minimap is dark with a white shop square, health is a white bar that turns red at 30% or less, armour is grey in two halves, the weapon panel has the 1/2/3 slots on top with the gun name and a big ammo count. **HUD** – there are no health bars over players' heads or over sentries, just their name, in their team colour (in free for all, the colour of their character); bottom left is a vitals panel with health, armour and stamina bars in that order, and above it a compact items row: an icon, its key and how many you have (consumable, walls, plus shop grenades and speed boots when you have them); on phones this row is hidden because the buttons show the same counts. Throwables on the item bar (grenade, stun, flash, mine, cluster, gold grenade) light up yellow whenever you have one ready, and go grey again while recharging or when you're out; walls and the sentry stay black and white.

**Volume** – Sound, Weapons, SFX and Music sliders are in the Options panel (the gear icon, top right of the front menu); they're the same settings as the ones on the pause menu. All four follow a curve, so the low end is very quiet (20% on the slider is about 2% volume). Sound is the master level for every sound effect. On top of it, Weapons sets only the weapons: gunfire, launchers, reloads, swaps, dry fire, near misses, the flamethrower, melee weapon swings and hits (fists, sword, bat), lethals (throws, flash, stun) and explosions from rockets, grenades and mines; moving it fires a test M4 shot. SFX sets everything else: footsteps, impacts, glass, barrels, cars, finishing moves, the shop and the like; moving it plays a test landing thud. So Sound at 50% and Weapons at 50% makes guns quieter than everything else, and SFX at 0% leaves only the weapons. Music has its own slider. They're saved in the browser.

**Live TVs** – every telly is switched on: the living-room TVs show an imok1 channel with a slowly panning landscape and a scrolling news ticker, and the den's screen plays IMOK FM with a scrolling track name and a bouncing equaliser (`liveTV` in `index.html`). **Pause menu** – Resume, Controls (every key, grouped, with **key rebinding** at the top on computers: click an action, press the new key; picking a key that is already used swaps the two, Esc cancels, P stays as pause, Reset keys puts them back; the written key list, the HUD items row and the menu hints follow your keys; touch buttons are unaffected), HUD layout and Home, plus settings: **Graphics** (Auto, Low, Med, High), **Frame rate** (Auto, 30, 60, Max; Auto is the default for anyone who hasn't picked one: uncapped on computers and 60 on phones, and if the game still can't hold about 45 fps once Auto graphics is as low as it goes (or graphics is set by hand) it settles on a steady 30, then about every 45 s tries the higher rate again for a few seconds and keeps it if it holds; the note under the settings and the FPS readout show what Auto is doing), **Auto weapon switch** (swap to your other gun when the one in hand runs dry; if the other gun is empty too, or you swap back to an empty gun, it reloads automatically; off by default), touch controls on/off, and on phones **Aim assist** (Off, On, Strong), **Aim when firing** (holding Fire also aims down sights) and **Gyro aim**. Sliders: camera sensitivity (up to 6×), **Aim sensitivity (other guns)** (0.1–1), **Sniper scope sensitivity** (0.1–2, HDR and Kar98), sound and music, plus **Battery saver** (30 fps and Low graphics in one tap) and **Show FPS** (a small frame-rate readout). HUD layout switches each panel on or off; **Move & resize** is where everything is placed and sized: drag any panel or control, tap it to make it bigger or smaller (60–160%) and more or less see-through (Opacity 10–100%, kill feed starts at 65%), and on phones an **All buttons** size; the edit bar can be minimised to a strip at the top. Your layout is remembered (phones keep their own layout, separate from desktop) and Reset layout puts it back. Below the volume sliders: **Field of view** (60–100°, 70 by default; aiming down sights still uses each gun's own zoom), **Brightness** (50–150%), **Crosshair** style (Cross + dot, Cross, T, Dot or Circle, with a live preview; guns with their own sight, such as the snipers' red dot and the rocket ring, keep it), **Crosshair colour** (white, green, yellow, cyan or pink; it still turns red over an enemy), **Crosshair size** (Small, Medium, Large), **Crosshair outline** (on/off), **Hit sound** and **Hitmarker** (each on/off), **Kill commentary** (the Double kill / Payback callouts, on/off), **Name tags** (the names over other players' heads, on/off), **Camera** (Third person or First person: in first person the view sits in your head with the gun or fists drawn at the bottom of the screen, held at the hip and raised to the sights when you aim; it switches back to third person for finishers on either side, going down, Man Down falls, ladders, and while the Sword or bat is out, so their swings still read) and **Reset all settings** (tap twice; puts sensitivity, volumes, graphics, touch and aim settings, these options, keys and the HUD layout back to the defaults and restarts the game; your level, records and menu picks stay). The **Options** cog on the home screen opens this same Settings screen, tabs and all (no Resume button there, and a **Controls** link in the tab rail for keys); Back, Esc or clicking outside returns to the menu. Slow motion only kicks in for moments you're part of (your own down, kill or finishing move), never for other players' finishers. XP is only shown on the menu and the end-of-match screen.

**Replays and Home** – clips are recorded from exactly what you see, scope included, at up to 1280×720 and 30 fps (cropped to 16:9, compressed in the background). Lower graphics settings record smaller, slower clips (Medium: 640 wide at 20 fps, 480 at 12 fps on phones; Low: no replays). Only the 4 best of the match are kept: the winning shot first, then finishing moves, headshots and quick double kills. Each clip keeps the moment that decided the fight: the shot that downed someone (from about 3 seconds before it to 1 second after) joined to the last 4.5 seconds before they're finally out (the finish or finishing move) and a moment after, so a long finisher or a downed opponent can't push the actual shot out of the clip. Clips are only shown at the end of the game, behind the video icon on the results bar. Watching a replay pauses the game; Save video downloads it as a video file. There's no floating Home icon any more: Home is on the pause menu and is a button beside Rematch on the end-of-match bar.

**Weapon weight** – the weapon in your hands sets how fast you move, for players and bots alike. An assault rifle is the baseline; Dual Pistols are 8% faster, SMGs 3–6% faster, shotguns and the Kar98-type snipers 4–5% slower, the HDR, Reaper and Titan 8–10% slower, the Flamethrower 10–12% slower and the Rocket Launcher 14% slower. Fists, the Bat and the Sword keep their +20%. Each gun's card in the loadout shows it as a Mobility bar.

**Fists and Sword** – with fists out the view goes first-person (like aiming a gun) and you see your own two fists in a boxing guard at the bottom of the screen, each jab shooting straight out to the crosshair, alternating left and right; everyone else sees your character hold a boxing guard (elbows bent, fists up by the chin) and each punch snaps one arm straight out along the aim, alternating left and right with the shoulders turning into it, so punches read clearly on every character. Melee has its own sounds: a quick air whoosh and a meaty body thud for fists, and a steel “shwing” and slice for the Sword.

**Damage** – every weapon hits 20% harder than it used to, so fights end a little quicker. On top of that, every primary and secondary gun (not fists, the sword or lethals) does 25% more damage per hit, rounded to whole numbers; the loadout cards show the new figures. Both boosts are now baked into the `GUNS` table in `index.html`, so the `dmg` values there are the real in-game numbers. **Balance pass (Nov 2026):** the AKM is the long-range AR (full damage out to 55 m, dropping to 45% by 130 m, 0.14 s between shots; 4 body shots in 0.42 s); the Shotty Horror's second barrel waits 0.45 s; the UMP hits for 18 (6 body shots); the Dual Pistols fire every 0.16 s and the Vector every 0.045 s, so secondaries no longer out-kill the primaries up close.

**Crossbow and breakables** – the Crossbow's bolt flies at 200 m/s with only a slight drop (it used to be 75 m/s, slow enough that a moving target had stepped out of the way before it arrived), leaving a faint green streak. Every weapon can break things: crossbow bolts smash glass, lamps and barrels, set off mines and knock loose items along their path; the Sword and Fists smash glass, barrels and other breakables at head or waist height when a swing meets no one; and the Flamethrower burns through glass and props and sets off barrels where its jet lands (guns and explosives already did).

**Reviving** – stand over a downed teammate and they glow green and revive twice as fast (2.5 seconds instead of 5).

**End of match** – "You caught the W!" or "Took the L this time :(" in big letters, the score and four stats: Kills, Damage, Accuracy, Headshots. Rematch and, if any clips were saved, a video icon (with the number of clips on it) that pops up beside it; tap it for the list of clips. Clips are only offered at the end of the game, never between rounds. If you pick nothing within 10 seconds, or 20 seconds after a win (a small "Home in Ns" countdown shows on the right), it takes you back to the home screen; clicking anywhere on the bar stops the countdown. Home is the button beside Rematch.

**Lethals** (what used to be called consumables; press E): the simple, powerful **Grenade** is back as the first option (one big 60-damage blast, 2 per round), then Cluster, Mine, Gold Armour, Stun and Flash. HeartBeat stays out of the loadout and Stim has been removed from the game completely.
- **Flash Bang** (2 per round) – thrown like a grenade; anyone who can see the blast gets a white screen for exactly 1 second including the fade-out (0.6 if facing away). Bots lose track of you for as long.
- **Proximity Mine** (2 per round) – laid on the floor in front of you, armed after a second (fast-blinking red light while arming), goes off when an enemy comes within about 1.7 m (60 damage); anyone can shoot it to set it off: a full mine blast with the same damage and radius, counted as the shooter's, so shooting an enemy's mine next to them hurts them, and you take half if you're inside the blast; it's dark grey with a dim red blink, so it's easy to miss. Other blasts set it off too.
- **Cluster Grenade** (2 per round) – a small burst that throws out five bomblets, each a smaller, weaker blast (24 damage) a moment later.
- **Gold Armour** (always on) – you start every round or respawn with 30 armour, which rebuilds back to 30 slowly once you've gone 3 seconds without being hit. Your armour bar (and the sparks when it soaks a hit) is gold instead of blue.

Menu descriptions are short: Stun "Stunned 1s", Flash "Blinded 1s", Mine "Proximity", Cluster "Multi BOOM!!!", Gold Armour "Armour + Rebuilds".

**Character motion** (looks only: aim, speed and hitboxes are unchanged) – legs are driven by where the feet should be: each foot plants and travels back at ground speed, then lifts and swings forward, in whichever direction you're moving (forwards, backwards or sideways), with a pelvis bob, a lean into the run and into starts and stops, a tuck in the air and a knee-bend on landing that grows with the fall. The chest shares the aim pitch through a waist joint (hips go the other way so the head stays over its hitbox), breathes at rest and flinches away from each hit, with the head snapping on headshots. Feet are planted at a point in the world while they are down, so they don't slide through turns, stops or changes of speed; each swing leaves and lands near ground speed; after a stop or a turn on the spot a foot that is out of place takes a small step back under the hip; knees ease into full stretch; the stride's shape follows a smoothed speed so running into a wall doesn't snap the pose; other fighters' bodies turn towards their aim over a few frames. The game simulates 60 times a second, and drawn positions (every body, and your camera with yours) are blended between steps so movement is even on 90, 120 and 144 Hz screens. Going down is a short collapse that settles into a prone crawl (arms pulling in turn, legs trailing). Shots at a downed player count if they hit the fixed downed box or the body you can actually see (head, chest, hips, knees and feet), so the collapse, a raised head or a body turned away from a wall can't make hits miss. Models get a rim light, a contact shadow under the feet, surface relief worked out from their own texture and a small brightness lift for very dark outfits. Per-character extras live in `CHAR_FX` in `index.html` (Wednesday has springy hair, a swinging trouser chain, loose hems and a blink). `CFX` switches each part off for testing.

**Deaths and celebrations** – an ordinary kill drops the body as a ragdoll (it crumples under its own weight, is shoved the way the killing shot or blast was travelling, and folds over or against whatever it lands on) with a few small red blocks round it (no explosion, no slow motion); only finishing moves blow people apart. Downing someone no longer slows the game either. If you finish a downed enemy while every one of their teammates is also down, the whole team is out. Winning a round gets a quick fist pump, a high five between teammates or a little jig, except when a finishing move ends the round: then there's no celebration and the next round starts sooner. Winning the match, in every mode including Free for all, shows the Victory banner for about a second and a half and then goes straight to the results scoreboard (the throne-room crown scene has been removed). Before any celebration or finishing move starts, anyone standing close to a wall, car, prop or other solid object first steps out into clear space (finishers slide both players into open room in under half a second), so nobody clips into or through anything. Round and match results sit in a slim bar across the top so the celebration stays in view. The kill feed sits under the score at 65% opacity.

**Explosive drums** – every Duels map (and any new one) gets a few small clusters of red fuel drums (one to three drums each, spread well apart), tucked beside walls, crates and cars on open ground, clear of spawns, shops, ladders, stairs, water and the train lines. Shoot one and it blows (and sets off its neighbours). They sit in the same spots each time you play a map.

**Bots get everywhere you can on foot** – bots climb ladders, jump and double-jump up onto crates, containers, ledges and roofs (onto a canopy roof like the shop they jump straight up beside the edge and step on once clear), and squeeze through tight gaps beside railings. A jump a bot keeps missing gets dropped from its routes for the rest of the match. Rooftop spawns are only placed where bots can climb to. The only spots they can't reach are ones you need Jet's rocket boost for (cliff-top rocks, the pavilion and signal-box roofs, the crane cab top, the platform shelter roofs).

**Bots pick the right gun for the range** – bots swap to whichever of their two guns suits the distance: shotguns, SMGs and other close-range guns when you're up close, rifles and snipers further out (never a rocket at point blank, and they think twice before swapping to an empty gun). How quickly and how reliably they do it depends on difficulty: Easy bots rethink every few seconds and often stay on the wrong gun, Normal ones every second or two, and Hard and Extreme ones swap almost as soon as the range changes.

**Bots under fire** – when a bot gets shot it picks a reaction instead of always walling up: dodging side to side with hops, ducking into cover, backing off, relocating round your side, walling up (less often) or just fighting on. Rushers and breachers mostly fight on.

**Neon Runners (speed shoes)** – one pair on every map, in every mode: detailed black knit trainers (glossy heel and toe caps, white midsole with glowing cyan air pockets, silver side flash, criss-cross laces) hovering and spinning over a glossy black hex pad whose honeycomb tiles ripple with light, with white chevrons racing round the rim, counter-spinning holo arcs and rising sparks. They sit away from the middle (the shop), spawns and walls, at a random spot each round, arriving 10 s after the round starts. Run over them for **8 s at +50% speed** (stacks with the shop's Speed) with a cyan light trail at your feet and a timer in the item bar; a new pair turns up somewhere else 30 s after they're taken. Going down ends the boost. Bots grab them when they're close and not mid-fight.

**Bat pickup** – in Rumble a glossy white bat with red grip tape and barbed wire stands spinning on a glowing red ring in the middle of the map each round, arriving 10 s after the round starts. Walk over it to take it: it replaces your fists (in Rumble that's your weapon; anywhere else it takes the slot-3 fists option, so a map can opt in later with `bat:1`). 50 a hit (2 hits to down), swings like the sword with the sword's sounds and trail, and knocks people back harder. Get downed and you drop it where you fall (back to the middle if that's off the map or in deep water); the round ending takes it away. Only one bat ever exists, and bots go and fetch it when it's nearer than the fight.

**Armour plates** – a few glowing blue armour plates sit on the hardest places to get to on each map (high roofs on Street Life, the top of the book tower on Toy Town); Rumble games have none, on any map, since Rumble has no armour. Plates always sit in different areas of the map from each other. Collect one for +50 armour (up to 100); it comes back 60 seconds later somewhere else (away from where it was and from the other plates), and every round start shuffles them to fresh hard-to-reach spots. A plate on a building that gets brought down moves elsewhere after the same 60 seconds. Armour doesn't carry over: everyone starts each round with none.

**Cover** – bullets go through wood (fences, sheds, furniture, wooden floors and crates; each layer takes off 30% of the damage, up to three layers) but not through concrete, brick, walls, cars or built walls. Bodies can't pass through solid structures: anyone pushed into a wall is moved back out.

At 0 HP you go down instead of dying, with 30 downed health that takes full damage, so a few shots finish you: you crawl slowly, and if nobody finishes you, you revive automatically after 5 seconds with whatever downed health you have left, and regen waits 3 seconds before it starts. Downed players can't punch (bots included). While down, clicking fire tags the enemy you're aiming at so they're outlined in red for 3 seconds. In 2v2, if you're down and your teammate is eliminated, you're out too. The opponent can walk up and press F for a finishing move picked at random each time from the four finishing moves, evenly split, never the same one twice in a row (except near a fence, where it's always Pancake). Once a finishing move starts it can't be interrupted: the one doing it can't be hurt until it's over, and firing doesn't cancel it. Every finishing move has its own sound effects; any punch in them (Heartbreaker, Combat's uppercut) uses the same landed-punch sound as fists. Combat (harpoon them in close, then uppercut) ends by throwing the body right out of the arena: off the roof on Sky High, into the sea on Lagoon, or out over the fences, picking a direction and enough height to clear whatever is in the way; it smashes or splashes where it lands. Finish someone close to the arena's fence, railing or rope and the move is always Pancake, and they go out over it (Pancake's last swing hurls them out instead of flattening them). Finishing moves (Nov 2026): there are four: Pancake, Heartbreaker, Roast and Combat. The decisive hit freezes for a split second with a white impact flash (fling landings, the Pancake slam, the Combat uppercut). Pancake picks one of three swing patterns (side to side, a helicopter spin overhead, or slams in front and behind), and the flattened body springs back into shape with a boing (no dust or rubble chunks flying off). Heartbreaker lasts 4 s and you hear the heartbeat slow and stop. Roast: the victim goes up in a dense, billowing blaze that clings to the body, with hot cores, a column of black smoke and a flickering firelight on the floor and anyone nearby (the light is always in the scene, switched off, so it never stalls the game); they run, flail, try stop-drop-and-roll, gets up to their knees and freezes as a charred, smoking statue with glowing cracks, then crumbles into an ash heap. Combat goes slow-motion as the spear hits and reels in with a ratchet. The ratchet and boing sounds are generated in the game. Every elimination plays the winner's kill effect: each character has two kill effects of their own. XP: +10 per round won, +5 per round lost, 100 XP per level. Built walls sink away after 6 seconds. Walls are easy to shoot down: a hit nudges a brick out along the shot, a broken brick is knocked out, drops and bursts when it hits the ground, any brick left with nothing under it falls too, and knocking out two bricks from the bottom row brings the whole wall down, toppling away from the shots.

The hip-fire crosshair is white and turns red when an enemy is under it, in clear view and within the weapon's range. Aiming down sights with any non-sniper gun switches to first person with see-through ring sights (bigger rings on ARs, small on pistols, huge on the rocket launcher; SMGs have real iron sights, below). Controls: WASD move · Space jump (press again in mid-air to double jump; it costs 15 stamina; you hear a soft push-off of air, `sfx/djump.mp3`, OpenGameArt 'Air Woosh Move', CC0) · mouse aim (click to lock) · left click fire (snipers: hold to scope, release to fire) · right click aim down sights / scope · C crouch (toggle; moving forward or jumping stands you up) · E lethal · T shop grenades (when your consumable isn't the grenade) · V put down a shop Sentry · F finish · R reload · 1/2/3 or wheel switch (primary, secondary, fists; wheel up goes to the next weapon, so from your primary up is your secondary and down is fists; fists are always there as a 3rd option and you move 20% faster with them out, except in Rumble where they are all you have; the touch Swap button cycles all three) · Q build wall (3 bricks high, 6 across) · P or Esc pause (controls and sensitivity sliders are on the pause menu). **Play a friend (online)** – on the front menu, open the **Play a friend** icon (top right); **Host game** gives you a 4-letter room code; your friend opens the game, types the code and presses **Join**. The friend's character and guns are whatever they pick on their menu; the host chooses the mode and map and presses Start, and the friend takes the place of the first enemy bot (in 1v1 you fight each other; in 2v2 and Free for all the other slots stay bots). The host's game runs the match; the friend's movement and shots show instantly on their screen and the host decides the hits. If the friend leaves or drops out, a bot takes over their fighter and they can rejoin with the same code. Connections go device to device (WebRTC via the free PeerJS matchmaking server); a few strict mobile or work networks may block the direct link.

**SMGs** (MP5, Uzi, Vector, P90, UMP, Sting) never take a scope, red dot or holo sight (iron sights only, and the shop Scope doesn't fit them) and aim down sights faster than any other gun (0.07 s), so they snap up instantly.

**Secondaries** are the MP5, Uzi, Vector, P90, Dual Pistols, Rocket Launcher and Sword.

**Vector** – one of the fastest-firing guns (about 22 rounds a second, 14 damage a bullet, 25-round mag, so it empties in about a second); almost no kick but the spread opens up fast if you hold the trigger. Hot-pink "Super V" lower under a flat-top gunmetal upper with a full rail, flip sights, weapon light, slotted shroud and brake, and a folding skeleton stock.

**P90** – 50-round bullpup (15 damage a bullet) with hip-fire almost as tight as aiming; slower to aim and a 1.3 s reload. Sculpted charcoal body with neon green trim, trigger and thumbhole cut-outs, a smoked see-through top mag showing the rounds lying crossways and an orange follower, a rounded ring sight and a flash hider.

**Shotty Horror** (replaces the AA-12) – a side-by-side double-barrelled shotgun (break action, top lever, twin gold triggers, cyan stock with a pink glow strip, gold bead sight): two shells, fired one after the other (0.45 s apart), then a 1.2 s reload. Each shell is 10 pellets of 13 damage (130 if they all land within 7 m; damage drops off to 45% by 20 m). It's a two-tap gun: a single shell tops out at 75% of their health, so it takes both barrels to down someone; only an absolutely point-blank shell (within 1.5 m, at least half the pellets hitting) downs in one.

**Phones** – landscape only (a "Turn your phone sideways" screen shows in portrait and pauses the match). Move stick on the left (it floats: put your thumb down anywhere on the left side below the top HUD and the stick appears under it, going home when you let go; a small dead zone stops a resting thumb creeping), drag anywhere to look (fast swipes turn further), and a thumb-reach arc of buttons round Fire: Aim, Jump, Crouch, Reload, Swap, Wall and Item, with Finish and Nade appearing only when you can use them; counts and cooldowns show on the buttons. Every button also turns the view if you slide your thumb off it, so a swipe that starts on Jump or Reload still aims. The phone layout is kept inside the notch and home-bar safe area, and the top HUD shrinks to fit narrower phones (the default layout was set on an 844px-wide phone); long opponent names wrap onto two lines. Item and Wall are bigger (52px). Tap a weapon slot at the top (1, 2 or 3) to switch straight to it. The Fire button shows your ammo (a white tag when it's low, Reload when empty) and a ring fills round it while you reload. On phones the health panel shows Armour only when you have some and Stamina only while it isn't full (the HUD editor still shows both), and it is a little narrower so it clears the weapon panel. Add the game to your home screen for full screen (Android also goes full screen and locks to landscape when a game starts; iPhone shows a one-time Add to Home Screen tip). Sounds, icons and three.js are cached for quick loading. Phones start on Medium graphics with a 60 fps cap; menus, pause and results screens drop to 20 fps to keep the phone cool. Small props aren't drawn far away on Medium and Low, and characters are built from about half as many pieces as before. Auto starts recent high-end phone GPUs on High, and after it has had to drop a level it tries one level higher again next time. Switching away from the app pauses the match. On phone menus the Start button floats in the corner.

**Parkour** – 12 obstacle courses with double jump, swings and moving platforms (switched off in the menu for now; the code is still there).

Static scenery is merged into a handful of meshes at load (instead of ~1,000 separate boxes, each with its own outline), which keeps the frame rate up on Maple Street.

Under the hood the match runs on a fixed 60-ticks-a-second clock on every device, player input reaches the game as one command per tick, shot spread uses a per-match seed, and the match state can be saved as a small snapshot: groundwork for online multiplayer.

It's a single static `index.html` (three.js and fonts load from CDNs), served by GitHub Pages. To play locally, open `index.html` in a browser.
